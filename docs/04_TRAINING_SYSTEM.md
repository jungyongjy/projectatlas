# Project Atlas

## Training System

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Status:** Finalized

**Purpose:** Define the complete training methodology that Project Atlas is built around. This document serves as the source of truth for all programming decisions.

---

# 1. Overview

Atlas is built around concurrent training.

The objective is **not** to maximize any single physical quality.

Instead, the system develops multiple qualities simultaneously while minimizing interference between them.

The primary adaptations are:

- Aerobic Capacity
- Running Economy
- Hypertrophy
- General Strength
- Athleticism
- Mobility
- Injury Resilience

Programming decisions should always preserve this balance.

---

# 2. System Philosophy

The Atlas training system is built around five rules.

## Rule 1 — Running Takes Priority

The primary measurable performance goal is improving the 2.4 km run.

Strength training exists to support running rather than compete with it.

Programming should avoid creating unnecessary fatigue that negatively impacts quality running sessions.

---

## Rule 2 — Preserve Muscle

Muscle mass is considered a long-term asset.

The program should preserve (and slowly improve) muscularity while improving endurance.

Large sacrifices in physique are unacceptable.

---

## Rule 3 — Recover Before You Adapt

Adaptation only occurs after recovery.

The program deliberately leaves recovery capacity unused rather than constantly training at maximum recoverable volume.

---

## Rule 4 — Every Exercise Must Earn Its Place

Every exercise included must justify itself through one or more of the following:

- High hypertrophy stimulus
- Running transfer
- Injury prevention
- Athletic development
- Movement quality
- Time efficiency

Exercises that fail this test should eventually be replaced.

---

## Rule 5 — Sustainability Wins

The best program is the one that is still being followed five years later.

# 5. Strength Philosophy

Strength supports:

- running economy
- joint health
- muscular maintenance
- force production
- injury prevention

Strength is **not** the primary performance metric.

Therefore:

- low fatigue
- high efficiency
- progressive overload

take priority over maximal loading.

---

# 6. Gym Design Principles

Gym sessions should:

✓ Finish within approximately 30 minutes.

✓ Use supersets.

✓ Minimize waiting.

✓ Minimize setup.

✓ Preserve running performance.

✓ Be enjoyable.

The gym should not reduce motivation to run.

# 10. Why Bulgarian Split Squats?

Bulgarian Split Squats replace Leg Extensions because they provide:

- unilateral stability
- quad development
- glute contribution
- hip stability
- balance
- athletic transfer

Although Leg Extensions isolate the rectus femoris effectively, the overall return on investment is lower within a limited training budget.

Given only ~30 minutes per session, compound unilateral work offers superior total adaptation.

---

# 11. Why Machine Compounds?

Atlas intentionally favors machine compounds over heavy barbell lifts.

Reasons:

Lower setup time

Lower technical demand

Lower injury risk

Lower fatigue

Faster progression

Better recovery

Improved adherence

These advantages outweigh the theoretical benefits of barbell specificity for this user profile.

---

# 12. Direct Arm Training

Atlas intentionally retains direct arm work.

Weekly volume:

Approximately six direct sets for:

- Biceps
- Triceps

Reason:

Arms contribute significantly to overall physique.

Removing direct work provides minimal recovery benefit while noticeably reducing hypertrophy stimulus.

Therefore, arm isolation remains.

---

# 13. Shoulder Philosophy

Shoulders remain largely unchanged.

Reason:

Current development is satisfactory.

The existing program provides:

- overhead pressing
- lateral raises
- rear delt work

This creates balanced shoulder development without excessive fatigue.

---

# 14. Plyometrics

Atlas includes low-volume plyometrics.

- tendon health
- explosiveness

without creating excessive fatigue.

# 21. Deload Strategy

Every 8–10 weeks.

Reduce:

Volume

Approximately 50%.

Maintain intensity where practical.

Purpose:

Reduce accumulated fatigue.

Restore motivation.

Improve long-term progression.

---

# 22. Peaking Strategy (IPPT)

Target Event:

Late September / Early October.

Timeline

### 8 Weeks Out

Normal programming.

### 4 Weeks Out

Increase race specificity.

### 2 Weeks Out

Reduce unnecessary gym fatigue.

### Race Week

Reduce volume.

Maintain intensity.

Remain fresh.

The objective is to arrive at the start line fully recovered rather than maximally trained.

Joint pain?

↓

↓

---

Feeling excellent?

↓

Follow the program.

Do not add extra work unnecessarily.

# 24. Guiding Philosophy

Atlas intentionally favors:

Good enough forever

over

Perfect for six weeks.

The training system is deliberately conservative because consistency produces greater long-term results than constant optimization.
