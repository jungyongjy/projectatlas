Race week is about arriving at the start line fully recovered — not maximally trained. The physiological work was done in the preceding months. This week is about preservation of fitness and dissipation of fatigue.

## The Tapering Philosophy

Training creates two things: fitness and fatigue. Fitness decays slowly (over weeks). Fatigue dissipates quickly (over days).

The taper exploits this asymmetry: reduce training volume to clear fatigue while maintaining enough intensity to preserve fitness. The result is an athlete who is **fresh** — not one who has gained fitness in the final week.

<InfoBox title="You cannot get fitter in race week">
The physiological adaptations from training take weeks to manifest. The final week before a race cannot improve your fitness. It can only improve your readiness to express the fitness you already have. Train to maintain, rest to perform.
</InfoBox>

## The 8-Week Build-Up

| Weeks Out | Focus |
|-----------|-------|
| **8** | Normal programming |
| **6** | Begin increasing race-specific quality work |
| **4** | Increase race specificity; maintain gym |
| **2** | Reduce unnecessary gym fatigue; prioritise running |
| **Race Week** | Reduce volume; maintain intensity; arrive fresh |

## Race Week Schedule

| Day | Activity | Notes |
|-----|----------|-------|
| **Monday** | Light gym or rest | If gym: upper body, low volume, no leg work |
| **Tuesday** | Short Zone 2 (3–4 km) | Maintain movement, no fatigue |
| **Wednesday** | Rest or very light mobility | Prioritise sleep and hydration |
| **Thursday** | Short run + 2–3 strides | Activate nervous system, stay sharp |
| **Friday** | Rest | Full recovery |
| **Saturday** | Rest or 10-minute shakeout jog | Optional — only if it feels good |
| **Sunday** | **Race Day** | Execute the plan |

<CoachNote>
The hardest part of race week is doing less. After months of consistent training, the impulse to "do one more hard session" is strong. Resist it. Trust the preparation. The work is already done — this week is about letting your body express it.
</CoachNote>

## Race Week Nutrition

| Priority | Guideline |
|----------|-----------|
| **Hydration** | 2–3 L water daily; consistent throughout the week |
| **Carbohydrates** | Normal intake; slight increase 1–2 days before if desired |
| **Protein** | Maintain normal intake (~140–160 g/day) |
| **Supplements** | Continue creatine and protein as normal |
| **Alcohol** | Avoid entirely in race week |
| **Race morning** | Light meal 2–3 hours before; banana or small snack 30–60 min before |

<WarningBox title="Do not experiment during race week">
Race week is not the time to try a new supplement, a new meal timing strategy, new shoes, or a new warm-up routine. Everything should be familiar. If it was not part of your preparation, it should not be part of your race.
</WarningBox>

## Race Day Execution

### Before the Race

1. **Wake up** — At your normal time (do not sleep in excessively)
2. **Hydrate** — Water; do not overdrink
3. **Eat** — Light, familiar food 2–3 hours before
4. **Warm up** — Standard dynamic warm-up + 2–3 light strides 15–20 minutes before start
5. **Mental preparation** — Review your pacing plan; visualise a controlled, strong finish

### Pacing Strategy

The 2.4 km is short enough that an even or slightly negative split is optimal.

| Segment | Pace Strategy |
|---------|--------------|
| **0–400 m** | Controlled — slightly slower than target pace |
| **400–1600 m** | Settle into target pace; stay relaxed |
| **1600–2000 m** | Maintain; this is where it gets hard |
| **2000–2400 m** | Empty the tank; whatever is left |

<InfoBox title="The first 400 m feel easy">
Do not be fooled. Adrenaline and fresh legs make the first lap feel effortless. If you go out too fast, you will pay for it in the final 800 m. The first 400 m should feel almost disappointingly slow. Trust the plan.
</InfoBox>

### After the Race

1. **Cool down** — 5–10 minutes walking + light jogging
2. **Rehydrate** — Water + electrolytes
3. **Eat** — Protein + carbohydrates within 1–2 hours
4. **Reflect** — Write down what went well and what to adjust next time
5. **Recover** — Take 2–3 easy days before returning to normal programming

<TipBox>
Your race result is not a judgement on your training. It is a data point. Win or miss your target, the same principles apply: review objectively, adjust calmly, and return to consistent training. One race is one data point in a lifelong process.
</TipBox>
