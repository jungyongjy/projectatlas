Zone 2 training is the foundation of the Atlas running system. It develops aerobic capacity, improves fat oxidation, and builds the physiological infrastructure that supports all faster running.

## Purpose

Zone 2 running improves:

- Mitochondrial density
- Capillary network development
- Fat oxidation capacity
- Lactate clearance
- Stroke volume
- Running economy at easy paces

These adaptations form the platform upon which speed work is built.

## Execution

Zone 2 should feel **conversational**. You should be able to speak in complete sentences without gasping.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Duration | ~30–45 minutes |
| Pace | ~7:00–7:30/km (current) |
| Target Pace | 6:30/km |
| RPE | 3–4 out of 10 |
| Heart Rate | ~140–150 bpm |

<InfoBox title="The Talk Test">
If you cannot hold a conversation, you are running too fast. Zone 2 is about building the engine, not testing it. Slow down.
</InfoBox>

## Benefits

Zone 2 provides the highest aerobic return for the lowest fatigue cost.

- Improves recovery between quality sessions
- Increases weekly mileage safely
- Builds mitochondrial density over months and years
- Enhances fat utilisation, sparing glycogen
- Reduces injury risk compared to faster running

## Common Mistakes

### Running Too Fast

The most common error. Athletes drift into Zone 3 because Zone 2 feels "too easy." This defeats the purpose — you accumulate fatigue without the specific adaptations of true Zone 2 training.

<CoachNote>
If you only have time for one change: slow down your easy runs. Most athletes run their easy days too fast and their hard days too slow.
</CoachNote>

### Skipping Zone 2

Some athletes believe easy running is wasted time. In reality, Zone 2 is where the aerobic engine is built. You cannot skip the foundation and expect the structure to stand.

### Racing Easy Runs

Zone 2 is not a test. There is no prize for finishing an easy run faster. The adaptation comes from time spent at the correct intensity — not from pace.

<ScientificNote>
Zone 2 training primarily stimulates Type I (slow-twitch) muscle fibres and enhances mitochondrial biogenesis through the PGC-1α signalling pathway. These adaptations occur gradually over months and years — they cannot be rushed with higher intensity.
</ScientificNote>

## Progression

Zone 2 improvement is measured by **pace at the same heart rate**.

Do not attempt to:
- Increase pace while increasing distance
- Add a fourth running day while increasing intensity
- Progress multiple variables simultaneously

Pick one variable and progress it conservatively over several weeks.
