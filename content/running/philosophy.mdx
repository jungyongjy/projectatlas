Running is the primary performance driver in Atlas. The strength programme exists to support running, not compete with it.

This page explains **why** the running system is structured the way it is. For the **what** and **how**, see the individual workout pages.

## Why Running Takes Priority

The primary measurable performance goal is the 2.4 km run. Every programming decision — strength, mobility, nutrition, recovery — is evaluated against its effect on running performance.

This does **not** mean running is the only thing that matters. It means running is the **measurable outcome** by which the system's effectiveness is judged.

<InfoBox title="Running as the North Star">
Having one clear performance metric simplifies every other decision. "Will this help or hurt my 2.4 km time?" is a question with a concrete answer. "Will this make me a better athlete?" is not.
</InfoBox>

## The Three Sessions

The running week is built around three distinct sessions, each with a different physiological purpose.

| Session | Day | Primary Adaptation | Intensity |
|---------|-----|-------------------|-----------|
| **Zone 2** | Tuesday | Aerobic base, mitochondrial density | Low |
| **Quality** | Friday | VO₂max, lactate threshold, economy | High |
| **Long Run** | Sunday | Endurance, fat oxidation, durability | Low–Moderate |

<CoachNote>
Three sessions is the minimum effective dose. Four would compromise gym recovery. Two would not provide sufficient stimulus for consistent aerobic improvement. This number was chosen deliberately — not by default.
</CoachNote>

## Progression Philosophy

Running progression follows a specific order. Never advance to the next stage before the current one is solid.

### The Progression Hierarchy

| Order | Variable | What It Means |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| **1** | Consistency | Complete every scheduled session for 4+ weeks |
| **2** | Technique | Cadence, foot strike, posture, arm drive |
| **3** | Economy | Reduced energy cost at a given pace |
| **4** | Pace | Faster splits at the same effort |
| **5** | Mileage | Increased weekly distance |

<WarningBox title="One variable at a time">
Never increase multiple variables simultaneously. Increasing pace while increasing distance while adding a fourth run is a recipe for injury. Progress one variable, consolidate for several weeks, then progress the next.
</WarningBox>

## The Quality Rotation

Friday quality sessions rotate through four distinct workout types on a four-week cycle.

| Week | Workout | Focus | Duration |
|------|---------|-------|----------|
| 1 | 6×400 m Intervals | Speed, VO₂max | ~30 min |
| 2 | Norwegian 4×4 | Maximal aerobic stimulus | ~35 min |
| 3 | Threshold Run | Lactate threshold | ~30 min |
| 4 | Strides Session | Running economy | ~30 min |

This rotation ensures every major running quality is developed. No single workout type is sufficient on its own — each addresses a different physiological system.

### Why Not Just One Type of Quality Work?

| If you only did... | You would develop... | But miss... |
|-------------------|---------------------|-------------|
| Intervals | Speed, anaerobic capacity | Sustained threshold, aerobic power |
| Threshold | Lactate clearance, race pace | Top-end speed, VO₂max |
| Strides | Economy, neuromuscular | Cardiac stroke volume, oxygen utilisation |
| Norwegian 4×4 | VO₂max, cardiac output | Speed endurance, running economy |

<ScientificNote>
Research on concurrent training demonstrates that rotating stimulus types produces superior overall adaptation compared to repeating the same workout structure. This is partly due to reduced monotony (improving adherence) and partly due to complementary physiological adaptations that compound over time.
</ScientificNote>

## Running and Strength: Managing Interference

Concurrent training creates interference. Running and lifting compete for recovery resources. Atlas manages this through scheduling and volume control.

### Scheduling

- Hard runs and hard gym sessions are separated by at least one sleep cycle
- Tuesday Zone 2 is easy — it does not interfere with Monday or Wednesday gym
- Friday quality runs are followed by Saturday gym (upper-body emphasis)
- Thursday is a complete rest day before the Friday–Sunday block

### Volume Control

- Gym sessions are approximately 30 minutes
- Lower body gym volume is intentionally moderate
- Leg training is programmed away from quality run days

<TipBox>
If you must shorten a session due to fatigue, shorten the gym session — never the run. The run is the priority. A shortened gym session still provides stimulus. A skipped run is a missed adaptation.
</TipBox>

## When Running Feels Hard

Every runner has difficult days. The skill is distinguishing between:

- **Normal fatigue** — train through it
- **Excessive fatigue** — reduce intensity
- **Pain** — stop, assess, modify

| Signal | Response |
|--------|----------|
| Heavy legs, normal pace feels harder | Normal — continue |
| Cannot reach normal Zone 2 heart rate | Reduce intensity |
| Sharp or localised pain | Stop running, assess |
| Consistent poor sleep + poor runs | Reduce volume for 1–2 weeks |
| Motivation low but body feels fine | Do the run anyway |

<CoachNote>
The difference between good runners and inconsistent runners is not talent or programme design. It is the ability to train appropriately on days when training feels difficult. Learning to distinguish "I don't want to" from "I shouldn't" is a skill — develop it.
</CoachNote>
