A proper warm-up is the cheapest injury insurance available. Five to ten minutes of dynamic preparation before every run improves performance and reduces injury risk.

## Purpose

The running warm-up serves four objectives:

1. **Increase tissue temperature**: Warmer muscles contract more efficiently and are less prone to strain
2. **Activate the nervous system**: Prime the neuromuscular pathways for running
3. **Mobilise key joints**: Ankles, hips, and thoracic spine need range of motion for efficient running
4. **Establish movement quality**: Reinforce good mechanics before fatigue sets in

<InfoBox title="Static vs. dynamic">
Static stretching (holding a stretch for 30+ seconds) before running reduces power output and does not reduce injury risk. Dynamic warm-up (moving through ranges of motion) prepares the body for running without these drawbacks. Save static stretching for after the run.
</InfoBox>

## The Atlas Running Warm-Up

Perform each movement for approximately 30 seconds. The entire warm-up should take 5–8 minutes.

| Order | Exercise | Purpose |
|-------|----------|---------|
| 1 | **Light Jogging** (2–3 min) | Raise body temperature, increase blood flow |
| 2 | **Leg Swings** (forward + lateral) | Hip mobility, dynamic hamstring length |
| 3 | **High Knees** | Hip flexor activation, cadence patterning |
| 4 | **Butt Kicks** | Hamstring activation, knee flexion range |
| 5 | **Dynamic Hamstring Swings** | Hamstring mobility under movement |
| 6 | **Ankle Rocks** | Ankle dorsiflexion range, calf preparation |
| 7 | **Walking Lunges** | Hip and quad mobility under load |
| 8 | **Calf Raises** (bodyweight, 15–20 reps) | Activate calves and Achilles complex |

<CoachNote>
The warm-up is not a workout. Do not fatigue yourself. The goal is to feel **prepared**, not **tired**. If you finish the warm-up breathing hard, you did too much.
</CoachNote>

## Quality Session Warm-Up

Before Friday quality runs (intervals, Norwegian 4×4, threshold), add 2–3 short strides at the end of the warm-up:

1. Complete the standard warm-up
2. Perform 2–3 × 50 m gradual accelerations to ~80% speed
3. Walk back between each

These "primer" strides prepare the nervous system for high-intensity work.

## What to Avoid

| Don't | Because |
|-------|---------|
| Static stretching | Reduces power output, does not prevent injury |
| Skipping the warm-up | Cold muscles at running intensity = heightened injury risk |
| Foam rolling before running | Better placed post-run or on rest days |
| Excessive warm-up | Creates unnecessary fatigue before the session begins |

<TipBox>
If you are short on time, do not skip the warm-up, **shorten the run instead**. A 20-minute run with a proper warm-up is better than a 30-minute run that starts cold and ends with an injury.
</TipBox>

## Weather Adjustments

| Condition | Adjustment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Hot weather** | Standard warm-up is sufficient |
| **Cold weather** | Extend light jogging to 5 minutes; consider warming up indoors |
| **Rain** | Standard warm-up, be mindful of slippery surfaces during dynamic work |
| **Early morning** | Extend warm-up slightly: body temperature is lowest upon waking |

<ScientificNote>
Research consistently demonstrates that dynamic warm-up protocols improve running economy by 2–3% in subsequent efforts, primarily through increased muscle temperature (improving contraction velocity) and neural activation (improving motor unit recruitment patterns). The effect size is small but meaningful, over a 2.4 km run, 2% economy improvement translates to approximately 14 seconds.
</ScientificNote>
