Threshold training improves the ability to sustain a fast pace before lactate accumulation forces deceleration. It is arguably the most specific training for the 2.4 km distance.

<img src="/images/running/threshold.svg" alt="Threshold run, a continuous comfortably hard effort" className="my-6 w-full max-w-md rounded-lg border border-atlas-border" />

## Purpose

Threshold running develops:

- Lactate clearance rate
- Sustainable pace at high intensity
- Running economy at race-relevant speeds
- Mental resilience during sustained discomfort
- Capillary and mitochondrial efficiency

These adaptations directly translate to faster 2.4 km performance.

## What Is Lactate Threshold?

Lactate threshold is the exercise intensity at which lactate begins to accumulate in the blood faster than it can be cleared. Below threshold, you can continue indefinitely (with adequate fuelling). Above threshold, fatigue accumulates and eventual deceleration is inevitable.

Threshold training raises the pace at which this accumulation begins, allowing faster running before fatigue becomes limiting.

<InfoBox title="Threshold in simple terms">
If Zone 2 is "I could do this forever" and intervals are "I can do this for 2–4 minutes," threshold is "I can do this for 20–40 minutes but I will be relieved when it ends."
</InfoBox>

## Execution

Threshold runs are performed at a "comfortably hard" effort.

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Duration** | 20–30 minutes of threshold effort |
| **Pace** | ~5:30–6:00/km (estimated) |
| **RPE** | 6–7 out of 10 |
| **Heart Rate** | ~85–90% of maximum |
| **Breathing** | Deep and rhythmic, but not gasping |
| **Talk Test** | Can speak 3–5 words at a time |

### Session Structure

1. **Warm-up**: 10 minutes easy jogging + dynamic stretches
2. **Threshold Block**: 20–30 minutes at threshold pace
3. **Cool-down**: 10 minutes easy jogging

<CoachNote>
The most common threshold error is starting too fast. The first 5 minutes should feel almost too easy. If you are struggling at minute 10, you started too hard and the session is compromised. Negative splitting (second half slightly faster than the first) is a sign of excellent pacing.
</CoachNote>

## Benefits

Threshold training provides the highest race-specific return for the 2.4 km distance.

- Improves the pace you can sustain for 10–12 minutes
- Develops the psychological skill of maintaining effort during discomfort
- Increases lactate clearance capacity
- Enhances running economy at race pace
- Provides a measurable benchmark for progress

## Progression

Threshold pace should improve gradually as aerobic fitness increases.

| Stage | Focus |
|-------|-------|
| **Beginner** | Complete 20 minutes at threshold without pace concern |
| **Intermediate** | Maintain consistent pace for 25 minutes |
| **Advanced** | Progress pace while maintaining 30-minute duration |

<InfoBox title="Measuring progress">
The best measure of threshold improvement is not a single session. It is the trend: is your threshold pace improving at the same heart rate? Track one threshold session per month on the same route for clean data.
</InfoBox>

## Common Mistakes

### Starting Too Fast

The most common error, and the most damaging. Starting above threshold pace forces deceleration in the second half. The session becomes a failed interval workout rather than a successful threshold session.

### Converting to a Race

Threshold is not a time trial. There is no prize for finishing faster than last time. The adaptation comes from time spent at the correct intensity, not from maximal effort.

### Skipping the Warm-Up

Threshold pace demands prepared muscles and an activated aerobic system. Starting cold produces poor-quality work and increases injury risk.

### Inconsistent Pacing

Threshold sessions require steady effort. Surging and recovering turns a threshold run into an accidental interval session and misses the specific adaptation.

<ScientificNote>
Threshold training primarily improves lactate clearance through increased monocarboxylate transporter (MCT) proteins and enhanced capillary density. These adaptations improve the muscle's ability to shuttle lactate from the cytoplasm into the mitochondria for oxidation, effectively turning a fatigue molecule into fuel.
</ScientificNote>
