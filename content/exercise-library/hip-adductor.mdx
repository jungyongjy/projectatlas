The Hip Adductor machine isolates the adductor muscle group — the muscles of the inner thigh. Adductor strength contributes to hip stability during the running gait cycle and is often undertrained in standard strength programmes that focus on the larger hip movers (glutes, quads, hamstrings).

## Purpose

Adductor (groin) strength and hypertrophy. Hip stability — adductors work with abductors to stabilise the pelvis during single-leg stance. Groin injury prevention.

## Muscles

| Role | Muscles |
|------|---------|
| **Primary** | Adductor magnus, adductor longus, adductor brevis, gracilis, pectineus |
| **Secondary** | Hip flexors (minor), obturator externus |

## Execution

1. Sit in the machine with the pads against the inner thighs
2. Select a weight that allows controlled movement through a full range
3. Squeeze the legs together against the pads
4. Control the return to the starting position

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 3 × 12–15 |
| **Range** | Full — legs apart to legs together |
| **Tempo** | Controlled; squeeze at peak contraction |

## Transfer Ratings

| Domain | Rating | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Running** | ★★★☆☆ | Adductor strength contributes to hip stability during stance phase |
| **Strength** | ★★☆☆☆ | Minimal direct carryover to compound lifts |
| **Athleticism** | ★★★☆☆ | Adductor strength supports lateral movement, cutting, and groin injury prevention |

<CoachNote>
The Hip Adductor machine is another "injury prevention" exercise — not glamorous, but valuable. Groin strains are common in athletes who run and change direction. Consistent adductor training reduces this risk. The machine provides isolated loading that free-weight exercises cannot easily replicate.
</CoachNote>
