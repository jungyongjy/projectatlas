The Hip Abductor machine isolates the gluteus medius and hip abductor muscles — critical for pelvic stability during the running stance phase. Weak hip abductors are associated with several running-related injuries, including IT band syndrome and patellofemoral pain.

## Purpose

Gluteus medius and hip abductor strength. Pelvic stability — preventing the hip from dropping on the opposite side during single-leg stance (the running gait position). Injury prevention.

## Muscles

| Role | Muscles |
|------|---------|
| **Primary** | Gluteus medius, gluteus minimus, tensor fasciae latae |
| **Secondary** | Piriformis, superior gemellus |

## Execution

1. Sit in the machine with the pads against the outer thighs
2. Select a weight that allows controlled movement through a full range of motion
3. Press the legs outward against the pads
4. Control the return — do not let the weight stack touch down between reps

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 3 × 12–15 |
| **Range** | Full — legs together to full abduction |
| **Tempo** | Controlled; do not bounce |

## Transfer Ratings

| Domain | Rating | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Running** | ★★★★☆ | Pelvic stability during stance phase is the gluteus medius's primary running function |
| **Strength** | ★★☆☆☆ | Minimal carryover to compound lifts |
| **Athleticism** | ★★★☆☆ | Hip stability supports cutting, lateral movement, and single-leg stance |

## Common Mistakes

### Too Much Weight

The hip abductors are relatively small muscles. Excessive weight forces the larger glutes and torso to contribute — reducing the specific stimulus to the target muscles.

### Bouncing Reps

Using momentum to bounce the weight outward rather than pressing with the hip abductors. Control the movement throughout the range.

<CoachNote>
The Hip Abductor machine may feel like a "minor" exercise. It is included because hip abductor weakness is a common contributor to running injuries, and the machine provides isolated loading that is difficult to replicate with free-weight exercises. Treat it as injury prevention — not optional.
</CoachNote>
