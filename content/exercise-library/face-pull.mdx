The Face Pull is widely considered the single most valuable shoulder health exercise. It targets the often-neglected rear deltoids and external rotators — muscles critical for shoulder stability and injury prevention that are chronically understimulated by pressing-dominant training and desk posture.

## Purpose

Posterior deltoid and external rotator (rotator cuff) strengthening. Scapular retraction and upward rotation. Shoulder health and injury prevention — the primary rationale for inclusion.

## Muscles

| Role | Muscles |
|------|---------|
| **Primary** | Posterior deltoid, infraspinatus, teres minor (external rotators) |
| **Secondary** | Rhomboids, middle trapezius, lower trapezius (scapular control) |

## Execution

1. Attach a rope handle to a cable pulley set at approximately upper chest height
2. Grip the rope with thumbs pointing back toward the face
3. Pull the rope toward the face, separating the hands and externally rotating the shoulders so the knuckles face backward at peak contraction
4. Squeeze for 1–2 seconds at peak contraction
5. Control the return

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 3 × 12–15 |
| **Load** | Light to moderate — this is a technique exercise, not a strength exercise |
| **Tempo** | 1–2 second squeeze at peak contraction, 2-second eccentric |

<CoachNote>
The Face Pull is not about weight. It is about activation and blood flow to underused tissues. If you cannot feel the rear delts and external rotators working, you are using too much weight or poor technique. Lighten the load until the movement feels precise and the target muscles fatigue before the larger back muscles.
</CoachNote>

## Common Mistakes

### Too Much Weight

The Face Pull is not a strength exercise. Loading it heavily forces the larger back muscles to take over and eliminates the specific external rotator and rear delt stimulus. Use a weight that allows 12–15 controlled reps with a clear contraction on every rep.

### Pulling to the Chest

The rope should come toward the face (as the name suggests) — not the chest. Pulling to the chest reduces external rotation and shifts emphasis away from the target muscles.

### Not Externally Rotating

The external rotation component — turning the knuckles backward at peak contraction — is what distinguishes the Face Pull from a generic row. Without this rotation, the exercise loses its specific benefit for the rotator cuff.
