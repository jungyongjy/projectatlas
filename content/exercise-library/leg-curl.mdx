## Overview

The Seated Leg Curl is a hamstring isolation exercise that develops the knee flexion function of the hamstrings with minimal systemic fatigue. It is superset with the Pull-Up in Full Body A and performed fresh so that hamstrings are trained before quad-dominant work.

## Purpose

Isolated hamstring hypertrophy and strength. The hamstrings function as both knee flexors and hip extensors. Leg Curls address the knee flexion role; Romanian Deadlifts address the hip extension role. Both are necessary for complete hamstring development.

## Execution

1. Adjust the machine so the pad sits just above the heels and the knee aligns with the machine's pivot point
2. Grip the handles for stability
3. Curl the weight by flexing the knees through a full range of motion
4. Squeeze the hamstrings at peak contraction
5. Lower under control (2–3 second eccentric)

## Common Mistakes

### Using Momentum

Swinging the weight up using hip movement rather than hamstring contraction. Reduce the weight and focus on controlled movement initiated by the hamstrings.

### Short Range of Motion

Partial reps that do not fully extend or fully flex the knee. Full range of motion maximises hamstring recruitment and hypertrophy stimulus.

### Excessive Weight

The Leg Curl is an isolation exercise. Loading it like a compound movement compromises form and increases injury risk at the knee joint. Prioritise contraction quality over load.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★★★☆☆.** Hamstring strength supports running propulsion and eccentric control of the knee during the gait cycle. Isolated knee flexion has moderate direct transfer; the hip extension role of the hamstrings is covered by the Romanian Deadlift. The Leg Curl is included because hamstring weakness is a common contributor to running-related knee issues.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Nordic Hamstring Curl** | Bodyweight alternative; excellent eccentric hamstring loading but higher demand and more difficult to progress |
| **Sliding Leg Curl** | Floor-based alternative using sliders or a towel on a smooth surface |

<CoachNote>
Hamstring strength is a common weakness and a contributor to running-related knee issues. Prioritise the Leg Curl within its superset rather than treating it as an afterthought. The eccentric lowering is where much of the hamstring stimulus lives, so keep it controlled.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
Eccentric hamstring training is associated with reduced risk of hamstring strain injury, a common injury in sports with sprinting and acceleration demands. While the Leg Curl is a concentric-first exercise, its controlled eccentric phase still provides meaningful hamstring strengthening within a low-fatigue, machine-based format.
</ScientificNote>
