The Romanian Deadlift (RDL) is the primary hip-hinge exercise in the Atlas programme. It develops the posterior chain — hamstrings, glutes, and spinal erectors — with lower systemic fatigue and technical demand than conventional deadlifts. The RDL emphasises the hip extension role of the hamstrings, complementing the Leg Curl (which addresses knee flexion).

## Purpose

Posterior chain hypertrophy and strength with emphasis on hamstring and glute development through a loaded hip hinge pattern.

## Muscles

| Role | Muscles |
|------|---------|
| **Primary** | Hamstrings (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus), gluteus maximus |
| **Secondary** | Erector spinae, adductors, latissimus dorsi (stabilising the bar) |

## Execution

1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, holding a barbell or dumbbells in front of the thighs
2. Soften the knees slightly — they should remain at a fixed angle throughout the movement
3. Hinge at the hips, pushing them backward while maintaining a neutral spine
4. Lower the weight along the front of the legs until a stretch is felt in the hamstrings (typically mid-shin level)
5. Drive the hips forward to return to standing

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 3 × 8–12 |
| **Range** | Lower until hamstring stretch is felt — typically mid-shin |
| **Knees** | Slightly bent, fixed angle throughout |
| **Spine** | Neutral throughout; avoid rounding |

<CoachNote>
The RDL is the most technically demanding exercise in the programme. Prioritise hamstring stretch and spinal position over load. A perfectly executed set at a moderate weight produces better results than a heavy set performed with a rounded lower back. If you only remember one cue: push the hips back, not the weight down.
</CoachNote>

## Transfer Ratings

| Domain | Rating | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Running** | ★★★★☆ | Hamstring and glute strength contribute to running propulsion; hip hinge pattern transfers to athletic movement |
| **Strength** | ★★★★☆ | Significant posterior chain loading with lower fatigue than conventional deadlifts |
| **Athleticism** | ★★★★☆ | Hip hinge is a fundamental athletic movement pattern; transfers to jumping, sprinting, and lifting |

## Common Mistakes

### Rounding the Lower Back

The most common and most consequential error. Lumbar flexion under load stresses the spinal discs. Keep the chest up, shoulders back, and maintain a neutral spine throughout the movement. If the lower back rounds, reduce the weight or reduce the range of motion.

### Bending the Knees Excessively

Excessive knee bend turns the RDL into a partial conventional deadlift, shifting emphasis from the hamstrings to the quadriceps. The knees should be soft but fixed — all movement occurs at the hips.

### Not Feeling Hamstring Stretch

If the hamstring stretch is not felt, the movement is either too shallow or the knees are bending too much. Push the hips further back and maintain the knee angle.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Conventional Deadlift** | Greater total body loading and systemic demand but higher fatigue, lower back stress, and technical complexity |
| **Kettlebell Swing** | Ballistic hip hinge; excellent for power development but harder to progressively overload |
| **Good Morning** | Barbell on back, hip hinge; effective but less commonly performed and more technically demanding |
| **45° Back Extension** | Bodyweight or loaded hip hinge with built-in spinal support; lower loading ceiling |

<ScientificNote>
The Romanian Deadlift produces comparable hamstring and glute hypertrophy to the Conventional Deadlift with lower spinal loading and systemic fatigue. For athletes whose primary performance goal is not maximal strength, the RDL offers a more favourable stimulus-to-fatigue ratio — particularly important in a concurrent training programme where recovery capacity is shared with running.
</ScientificNote>
