## Overview

The Seated Lateral Raise is the only exercise that effectively isolates the medial deltoid, the muscle responsible for shoulder width and the V-taper appearance. It is included in both Full Body A and Full Body B, providing twice-weekly frequency for a muscle group that responds well to higher training frequency.

<img src="/images/exercise/lateral-raise.svg" alt="Seated lateral raise, front view" className="my-6 w-full max-w-md rounded-lg border border-atlas-border" />

## Purpose

Medial deltoid hypertrophy. Shoulder width, a key component of the athletic upper body aesthetic.

## Execution

1. Sit with feet shoulder-width apart, holding dumbbells at the sides
2. With a slight forward lean and soft elbows, raise the dumbbells out to the sides
3. Lead with the elbows; think "pour the pitchers" at the top (thumbs slightly lower than pinkies)
4. Raise to approximately shoulder height, no higher
5. Lower under control

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 2 × 8–10 |
| **Load** | Light: the medial deltoid is a small muscle with poor leverage |
| **Range** | Arms at sides to approximately shoulder height |
| **Tempo** | Controlled; 2-second eccentric |

## Common Mistakes

### Too Much Weight

The most common Lateral Raise error. Excessive weight forces momentum (swinging), trapezius dominance (shrugging), and reduced range of motion. The medial deltoid is a small muscle; the weight should reflect that.

### Shrugging the Shoulders

The upper trapezius attempts to assist when the weight is too heavy or technique is poor. Keep the shoulders depressed throughout the movement. If the neck and upper traps fatigue before the deltoids, reduce the weight.

### Going Too High

Raising the dumbbells above shoulder height shifts emphasis away from the medial deltoid and toward the upper trapezius. Stop at shoulder height. Higher is not better.

### Straight Arms

Keeping the elbows completely straight increases the lever arm and joint stress on the elbow. Maintain a slight bend in the elbows throughout the movement.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★☆☆☆☆.** The Seated Lateral Raise has no direct transfer to running performance and minimal contribution to compound lift strength. It is included twice weekly for shoulder width as part of the programme's physique goals, using a low-fatigue isolation format that does not compromise running recovery.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Cable Lateral Raise** | Constant tension throughout the range of motion; unilateral version addresses asymmetries |
| **Machine Lateral Raise** | Fixed movement path; easier to standardise and progress |

<CoachNote>
The Lateral Raise is the exercise most commonly performed with too much weight. If you are using momentum, swinging the torso, or cannot control the eccentric, the weight is too heavy. The medial deltoid is a small muscle; it does not need heavy loading to grow. It needs consistent, high-quality volume with precise form.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
The medial deltoid is a relatively small muscle that responds well to moderate-volume, high-frequency training. Because it is frequently under-stimulated in pressing-heavy programmes, dedicated isolation at least twice weekly produces reliable shoulder-width gains without the systemic fatigue of heavier shoulder work.
</ScientificNote>
