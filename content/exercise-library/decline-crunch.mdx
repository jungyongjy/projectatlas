## Overview

The Decline Crunch is the programme's primary spinal flexion exercise. The decline bench increases the range of motion and resistance relative to a floor crunch, allowing the rectus abdominis to be loaded through a full contraction without the hip flexor involvement of sit-ups. It is performed as the first exercise of the core and posterior chain superset in Full Body A.

<Figure name="declineCrunch" />

## Purpose

Rectus abdominis hypertrophy and strength through spinal flexion. A low-fatigue core movement that builds visible and functional anterior core development without the systemic cost of heavy loaded core work.

## Execution

1. Set the bench to a decline angle of approximately 30–40°
2. Sit on the bench, hooking the feet under the ankle pads
3. Place the hands across the chest or lightly at the temples (do not pull on the neck)
4. Curl the upper back off the bench, bringing the ribs toward the pelvis
5. Lower under control to the starting position, keeping the movement in the spine rather than the hips

## Common Mistakes

### Pulling on the Neck

Using the hands to yank the head forward. Keep the hands across the chest or lightly at the temples, and let the abs do the work. If you need to pull, the weight or range is too ambitious.

### Using Momentum

Rocking or bouncing to generate upward momentum. Each rep should be a deliberate curl; the eccentric lowering is where much of the stimulus lives.

### Incomplete Range

Stopping short of a full crunch at the top or not lowering far enough to load the stretched position. Use the full available range of the decline bench.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★★☆☆☆.** Direct spinal flexion has limited transfer to running, where the core's primary role is anti-rotation and force transfer. The Decline Crunch is included for balanced core development and physique; the anti-rotation demands of running are covered by the Pallof Press.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Cable Crunch** | Constant tension through the range; easy to load progressively |
| **Weighted Sit-Up** | Greater hip flexor involvement; less isolated spinal flexion |
| **Hanging Leg Raise** | Anti-extension and hip flexor emphasis; more advanced |

<CoachNote>
The Decline Crunch is a quality-over-quantity movement. Fifteen controlled reps with a full range of motion beat thirty rushed partials. If you can comfortably complete 15 reps with good form, add load rather than chasing higher rep counts; the goal is progressive tension on the abs, not endurance.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
Spinal flexion exercises that keep the pelvis fixed and the lumbar spine moving through its neutral-to-flexed range load the rectus abdominis effectively while minimising hip flexor involvement. Compared with full sit-ups, which are dominated by the hip flexors and carry higher lumbar loading, the decline crunch offers a more isolated abdominal stimulus.
</ScientificNote>
