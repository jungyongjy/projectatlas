## Overview

The Hammer Curl is a neutral-grip (palms facing each other) curl variation that emphasises the brachioradialis (the muscle running from the upper arm to the forearm) and the brachialis, while still training the biceps. It complements the Preacher Curl, which emphasises the biceps in a supinated grip, for complete arm development.

## Purpose

Brachioradialis and brachialis hypertrophy. Biceps development with a neutral grip and reduced wrist stress compared with supinated curls. Complete arm development.

## Execution

1. Stand holding dumbbells at the sides, palms facing each other (neutral grip)
2. Keeping the elbows pinned to the sides, curl the dumbbells upward
3. Squeeze at the top of the movement
4. Lower under control to full arm extension

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 2 × 8 |
| **Grip** | Neutral (palms facing each other throughout) |
| **Elbows** | Pinned to the sides; do not allow them to drift forward |

## Common Mistakes

### Swinging the Dumbbells

Using body momentum to initiate the curl. Keep the torso still; all movement should come from elbow flexion.

### Elbows Drifting Forward

Allowing the elbows to move forward turns the curl into a partial front raise, reducing biceps and brachioradialis involvement. Keep the elbows pinned to the sides.

### Partial Reps

Not lowering to full arm extension or not curling to a full squeeze. Full range of motion is required for complete arm development.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★☆☆☆☆.** The Hammer Curl has no direct transfer to running performance. It is included for arm and forearm development as part of the programme's physique goals, complementing the Preacher Curl with a neutral-grip variation that spares the wrists.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Preacher Curl** | Supinated grip; greater biceps emphasis and long-length tension |
| **Dumbbell Curl** | Supinated or neutral; more stabiliser demand |
| **Cable Curl** | Constant tension; easy to adjust load |

<CoachNote>
The Hammer Curl is often performed as an afterthought at the end of a session. Treat it with the same attention as any other exercise. The brachioradialis contributes significantly to forearm size and grip strength. Well-developed forearms create the appearance of complete arm development, not just biceps.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
The brachioradialis is a large forearm muscle that contributes meaningfully to elbow flexion, particularly in the neutral and pronated grip positions. Including a neutral-grip curl alongside supinated curls provides more complete elbow flexor development than either variation alone.
</ScientificNote>
