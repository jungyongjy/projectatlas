The Hammer Curl is a neutral-grip (palms facing each other) curl variation that emphasises the brachioradialis (the muscle running from the upper arm to the forearm) and the brachialis, while still training the biceps. It complements the Preacher Curl (which emphasises the biceps in a supinated grip) for complete arm development.

## Purpose

Brachioradialis and brachialis hypertrophy. Biceps development with a neutral grip — reduced wrist stress compared to supinated curls. Complete arm development.

## Muscles

| Role | Muscles |
|------|---------|
| **Primary** | Brachioradialis, brachialis, biceps brachii |
| **Secondary** | Forearm flexors (grip) |

## Execution

1. Stand holding dumbbells at sides, palms facing each other (neutral grip)
2. Keeping the elbows pinned to the sides, curl the dumbbells upward
3. Squeeze at the top of the movement
4. Lower under control to full arm extension

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 3 × 8–12 |
| **Grip** | Neutral (palms facing each other throughout) |
| **Elbows** | Pinned to sides — do not allow them to drift forward |

## Common Mistakes

### Swinging the Dumbbells

Using body momentum to initiate the curl. Keep the torso still — all movement should come from elbow flexion.

### Elbows Drifting Forward

Allowing the elbows to move forward turns the curl into a partial front raise, reducing biceps and brachioradialis involvement. Keep the elbows pinned to the sides.

<CoachNote>
The Hammer Curl is often performed as an afterthought at the end of a session. Treat it with the same attention as any other exercise. The brachioradialis contributes significantly to forearm size and grip strength. Well-developed forearms create the appearance of complete arm development — not just biceps.
</CoachNote>
