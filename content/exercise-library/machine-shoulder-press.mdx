## Overview

The Machine Shoulder Press provides overhead pressing for deltoid, triceps, and upper chest development in a stable, controlled movement path. It removes the balance and technical demands of free-weight pressing, allowing focus on muscular stimulus without joint stress.

## Purpose

Anterior and medial deltoid hypertrophy and strength. Supports upper body pressing strength, shoulder aesthetics, and overhead stability.

## Execution

1. Adjust the seat height so the handles are at shoulder level
2. Grip the handles with a neutral or pronated grip
3. Press overhead until arms are extended but not locked out
4. Lower under control to the starting position

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 2 × 10 |
| **Range** | Full: handles to shoulder level at bottom, near-full extension at top |
| **Tempo** | Controlled; do not bounce at the bottom |

## Common Mistakes

### Excessive Weight

Loading the machine heavily and performing partial reps. Reduce the weight and use full range of motion; the handles should come down to approximately shoulder level on each rep.

### Locking Out Elbows

Fully locking the elbows at the top transfers load from muscles to joint structures. Stop just short of full lockout.

### Rounded Shoulders

Allowing the shoulders to round forward during the press. Keep the shoulder blades retracted and the chest up throughout the movement.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★☆☆☆☆.** Shoulder strength is not a limiting factor in running performance. The Machine Shoulder Press is included for balanced upper body development and shoulder health rather than running transfer, and it delivers those benefits with a favourable stimulus-to-fatigue ratio.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Barbell Overhead Press** | Greater stabiliser demand and loading potential; higher technical demand and injury risk |
| **Dumbbell Shoulder Press** | Unilateral loading exposes asymmetries; requires more setup and stability |
| **Lateral Raise** | Isolation alternative targeting the medial deltoid specifically |

<CoachNote>
The shoulder press is included for balanced upper body development and shoulder health, not because it improves running performance. Well-developed shoulders contribute to the overall athletic appearance that is part of the programme's physique goals. The exercise is low-risk, easy to progress, and efficient; it earns its place.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
Machine-based pressing produces comparable deltoid hypertrophy to free-weight pressing when volume is equated, with lower stabiliser demand but also lower injury risk and technical requirements. For a programme that must balance strength, running, and recovery, the machine press offers a favourable trade-off.
</ScientificNote>
