The Cable Row is a horizontal pulling exercise that develops mid-back thickness, scapular retraction strength, and postural control. Cable resistance provides constant tension throughout the range of motion — a significant advantage over free-weight rows where tension varies with leverage.

## Purpose

Mid-back (rhomboids, middle trapezius) hypertrophy and scapular retraction strength. Supports posture during running — particularly important as fatigue accumulates during longer efforts.

## Muscles

| Role | Muscles |
|------|---------|
| **Primary** | Latissimus dorsi, rhomboids, middle trapezius |
| **Secondary** | Biceps brachii, posterior deltoid, forearm flexors (grip), erector spinae (stabilising) |

## Execution

1. Sit at the cable station with feet braced, knees slightly bent
2. Grip the handle (close-grip or wide bar) with arms extended
3. Pull the handle toward the lower abdomen, driving the elbows back and squeezing the shoulder blades together
4. Control the return to full arm extension — allow the scapulae to protract slightly at the end

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 3 × 8–12 |
| **Grip** | Neutral (close-grip handle) or pronated (wide bar) |
| **Range** | Full — arms extended to handle touching torso |
| **Tempo** | 1-second squeeze at peak contraction, 2-second eccentric |

## Transfer Ratings

| Domain | Rating | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Running** | ★★★☆☆ | Scapular retraction strength supports upright running posture — particularly during late-race fatigue when form degrades |
| **Strength** | ★★★☆☆ | Moderate; cable resistance limits maximal loading compared to barbell rows |
| **Athleticism** | ★★★☆☆ | Horizontal pulling strength transfers to climbing and pulling movements |

## Common Mistakes

### Using Momentum

Rocking the torso to generate momentum rather than pulling with the back muscles. Keep the torso stable — movement should come from the arms and scapulae, not spinal flexion and extension.

### Shoulders Elevated

Shrugging the shoulders toward the ears during the pull. Keep the shoulders depressed — the pull should come from the elbows driving back, not the upper traps lifting.

### Incomplete Retraction

Not squeezing the shoulder blades together at peak contraction, missing the primary benefit of the exercise. Each rep should include a deliberate scapular squeeze.

<CoachNote>
The Cable Row is about quality, not load. If you cannot feel your mid-back working, reduce the weight and focus on the scapular squeeze. The mind-muscle connection with the rhomboids and middle traps is essential — these muscles are often underactive in individuals who sit at a desk all day.
</CoachNote>

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Barbell Row** | Greater loading potential; higher lower back demand; free-weight alternative |
| **Dumbbell Row** | Unilateral — addresses side-to-side asymmetries; requires bench for support |
| **T-Bar Row** | Leverage-based; heavy loading with less lower back demand than barbell row |
