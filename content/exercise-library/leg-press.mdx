## Overview

The Leg Press is the primary quad-dominant compound in the Atlas programme. It provides heavy lower body loading with minimal axial fatigue, no balance demand, and simple progression, making it ideal for a concurrent training programme where running performance is the priority.

<img src="/images/exercise/leg-press-foot-position.svg" alt="Leg Press foot position: shoulder-width and mid-platform" className="my-6 w-full max-w-md rounded-lg border border-atlas-border" />

## Purpose

Quadriceps and glute hypertrophy and strength. The Leg Press allows heavy loading of the lower body without the spinal compression, technical complexity, or recovery cost of barbell squats.

## Execution

1. Sit in the machine with back and hips firmly against the pads
2. Place feet shoulder-width apart on the platform, toes slightly turned out
3. Unrack the weight and lower the platform until knees reach approximately 90° (or slightly deeper if comfortable)
4. Press through the full foot, not just the toes or heels
5. Drive back to starting position without locking out the knees

## Common Mistakes

### Shallow Reps

Quarter-repping the Leg Press, heavy weight through a short range of motion. This reduces quadriceps stimulus while increasing joint stress. Reduce the weight and use full range of motion.

### Locking Out Knees

Fully extending and locking the knees at the top of each rep places the load on the joint structures rather than the muscles. Stop just short of lockout.

### Heels Lifting

Pressing through the toes rather than the full foot. Keep heels planted throughout the movement. If heels lift, reduce the weight or improve ankle mobility.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★★★☆☆.** Quad and glute strength support the force production required during running. Specificity is limited by the bilateral, seated position, which does not challenge the single-leg stability demands of the running gait. The Leg Press earns its place as the primary lower body compound because it delivers heavy loading with minimal fatigue, preserving recovery for running.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Barbell Back Squat** | Greater athletic transfer and stabiliser demand but significantly higher fatigue, technical demand, and injury risk for this athlete |
| **Hack Squat** | Machine alternative with more upright posture; greater quad emphasis |
| **Goblet Squat** | Dumbbell alternative; limited by grip strength and available dumbbell weight |

<CoachNote>
The Leg Press replaces the Barbell Back Squat in this programme. The decision is practical: the athlete dislikes squatting, the squat creates disproportionate fatigue, and the Leg Press provides comparable quad and glute development with lower recovery cost. For a hybrid athlete whose primary goal is running, this trade-off is correct.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
Research comparing machine-based and free-weight lower body training shows comparable hypertrophy when volume and effort are equated. Free-weight squatting produces greater trunk and stabiliser muscle activation but at the cost of greater systemic fatigue, an important consideration for athletes who must also run. For a concurrent training programme, machine compounds offer a favourable stimulus-to-fatigue ratio.
</ScientificNote>
