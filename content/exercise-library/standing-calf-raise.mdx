The Standing Calf Raise is the primary calf exercise in the Atlas programme. The calf-Achilles complex is the body's primary spring during running — it stores and returns elastic energy with each foot strike. Stronger, stiffer calves improve running economy: less energy is required to maintain any given pace.

## Purpose

Gastrocnemius and soleus hypertrophy and strength. Achilles tendon stiffness. Running economy improvement through enhanced force return. Calf injury prevention.

## Muscles

| Role | Muscles |
|------|---------|
| **Primary** | Gastrocnemius (medial and lateral heads), soleus |
| **Secondary** | Tibialis posterior, peroneals (stabilising the ankle) |

## Execution

1. Stand on the machine with the balls of the feet on the platform, heels hanging off
2. Position the pads on the shoulders (standing machine) or hold a dumbbell (free-standing)
3. Lower the heels as far as comfortable — feel the stretch through the calves and Achilles
4. Press through the balls of the feet, raising the heels as high as possible
5. Hold the peak contraction briefly
6. Lower under control

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 3 × 12–20 |
| **Range** | Full — deep stretch at bottom, full contraction at top |
| **Tempo** | 2–3 second eccentric; pause at bottom (stretch) and top (contraction) |

<CoachNote>
Calves respond to volume and frequency, not heavy loading. A set of 12–20 controlled reps with a deep stretch and full contraction at each end produces better results than heavier weight with partial range of motion. The eccentric (lowering) phase is where most of the adaptation occurs — do not rush it.
</CoachNote>

## Common Mistakes

### Short Range of Motion

Partial reps that skip the bottom stretch (lost eccentric stimulus) or the top contraction (lost concentric stimulus). Full range of motion — deep stretch to full contraction — on every rep.

### Bouncing

Using the stretch reflex at the bottom to bounce into the next rep. This reduces the stimulus and increases Achilles stress. Pause briefly at the bottom before pressing up.

### Excessive Weight

The calves handle heavy loads daily (bodyweight × several times during running). However, training them with excessive weight on the Standing Calf Raise encourages bouncing and partial range of motion. Moderate weight, full range, high quality.

<ScientificNote>
The calf muscles and Achilles tendon function as a series elastic component during running — they stretch under load during early stance (storing elastic energy) and recoil during late stance (returning that energy). This mechanism can provide up to 50% of the energy required for each stride in trained runners. Calf training improves both the strength of the contractile component (the muscles) and the stiffness of the series elastic component (the tendon), resulting in improved running economy.
</ScientificNote>
