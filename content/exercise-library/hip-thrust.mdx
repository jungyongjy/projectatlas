## Overview

The Barbell Hip Thrust is the primary glute isolation exercise in the Atlas programme. It provides high glute activation with minimal fatigue, directly targeting the primary hip extensor used during running. It pairs with the Weighted Back Extension to cover the posterior chain with two distinct, low-fatigue movements.

## Purpose

Direct gluteus maximus hypertrophy and strength. The glutes are the primary hip extensor during running; stronger glutes contribute to running propulsion and pelvic stability.

## Execution

1. Sit on the floor with upper back against a bench, knees bent, feet flat on the floor approximately shoulder-width apart
2. Place a barbell or dumbbell across the hips (use a pad for comfort)
3. Drive through the heels, extending the hips upward until the body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees
4. Squeeze the glutes at the top, avoiding hyperextension of the lower back
5. Lower under control to just above the floor

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 2 × 10 |
| **Loading** | Barbell across hips (with pad), dumbbell, or bodyweight |
| **Range** | Full hip extension without lumbar hyperextension |
| **Tempo** | 1–2 second squeeze at top, 2-second eccentric |

## Common Mistakes

### Hyperextending the Lower Back

Driving the hips too high by arching the lower back rather than squeezing the glutes. The body should form a straight line from shoulders to knees, not an arch. Focus on glute squeeze, not height.

### Feet Too Far Forward or Back

Feet too far forward emphasises hamstrings; too far back emphasises quadriceps. The optimal position places the knees at approximately 90° at the top of the movement.

### Rushing the Movement

The Hip Thrust benefits from a deliberate pause and squeeze at the top. Do not bounce through reps; each rep should include a clear glute contraction at peak hip extension.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★★★★★.** The glutes are the primary hip extensor during running; stronger glutes improve propulsion and pelvic stability. The Barbell Hip Thrust is the most direct way to train the glutes at peak contraction, and its low-fatigue profile preserves recovery for running.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Glute Bridge** | Floor-based version; shorter range of motion but easier to set up and less equipment-dependent |
| **Cable Kickback** | Unilateral glute isolation; lower loading potential but effective for activation and mind-muscle connection |
| **Weighted Back Extension** | Posterior chain emphasis with more spinal erector involvement; complements the Hip Thrust |

<CoachNote>
The Hip Thrust may feel awkward initially, particularly in a commercial gym. It is worth persisting. The glute activation produced by this exercise is difficult to replicate with any other movement, and the direct transfer to running propulsion makes it one of the most valuable exercises in the programme.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
EMG studies consistently demonstrate that the Barbell Hip Thrust produces significantly higher gluteus maximus activation than squats, deadlifts, and lunges, exercises traditionally considered primary glute developers. This is because the Hip Thrust places the glutes in a position of maximal mechanical advantage at peak contraction (full hip extension), which compound lifts do not achieve due to their loading vectors.
</ScientificNote>
