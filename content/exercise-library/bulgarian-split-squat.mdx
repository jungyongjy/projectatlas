## Overview

The Bulgarian Split Squat is the highest-ROI lower body exercise in the Atlas programme. It develops unilateral quad and glute strength, hip stability, and balance, all of which transfer directly to running performance. It replaced Leg Extensions, which provided effective quad isolation but minimal overall athletic transfer.

## Purpose

Unilateral lower body strength and hypertrophy with significant stability and balance demands. Addresses side-to-side asymmetries that bilateral exercises mask. Direct transfer to the single-leg stance phase of running.

## Execution

1. Stand facing away from a bench, approximately 60–90 cm in front
2. Place the top of one foot on the bench behind you
3. Position the front foot far enough forward that the front knee stays behind the toes during the descent
4. Lower until the front thigh is approximately parallel to the ground (or as deep as comfortable)
5. Drive through the front foot to return to the starting position
6. Complete all reps on one leg before switching

## Common Mistakes

### Front Foot Too Close to Bench

Placing the front foot too close forces the knee excessively forward over the toes, increasing knee stress and reducing glute contribution. Move the front foot forward until the knee tracks naturally over the midfoot.

### Rear Foot Position

The rear foot should rest on the bench with the top of the foot (laces) down. Placing the toe or ball of the foot on the bench creates instability. Top of the foot down, ankle relaxed.

### Rushing the Movement

Bulgarian Split Squats require balance. Rushing through reps compromises stability and reduces muscle activation. Control each rep; a 2–3 second eccentric is ideal.

### Not Matching Reps

Always start with the weaker leg and match reps on the stronger side. Doing more reps on the stronger leg widens the asymmetry.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★★★★★.** The Bulgarian Split Squat is the most running-specific lower body exercise in the programme. Single-leg strength, hip stability, and balance are all critical for the running gait, where every step is a single-leg stance. It also exposes and corrects the side-to-side asymmetries that bilateral exercises hide.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Lunges** | Similar unilateral stimulus; less stability demand; easier to load heavily |
| **Step-Ups** | Unilateral with less balance demand; easier to standardise |
| **Leg Extensions** | Quad isolation without stability or glute contribution; lower overall ROI but useful if knee pain prevents split squat execution |

<CoachNote>
The Bulgarian Split Squat will expose and eventually correct side-to-side imbalances. It is common for one leg to feel significantly weaker or less stable. This is not a problem to avoid; it is precisely why the exercise is in the programme. The imbalance was always there; the exercise is simply revealing it.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
Unilateral lower body training improves running economy through several mechanisms: increased single-leg force production capacity, improved hip and knee stability during the stance phase of gait, and reduced between-limb asymmetry, which is associated with both improved performance and reduced injury risk in runners.
</ScientificNote>
