## Overview

The Preacher Curl is a biceps isolation exercise performed on an angled bench that eliminates momentum and enforces strict form. The preacher bench positions the arms in front of the body, placing the biceps under maximum tension at the bottom of the movement where the muscle is fully stretched.

<Figure name="preacherCurl" />

## Purpose

Biceps brachii hypertrophy. Strict isolation with minimal momentum. The preacher bench angle maximises tension at long muscle lengths, a potent hypertrophy stimulus.

## Execution

1. Adjust the seat so the armpits rest comfortably against the top of the pad
2. Grip the barbell or EZ-bar with hands shoulder-width apart, palms facing up
3. Curl the weight upward until the biceps are fully contracted
4. Lower under control to full arm extension; do not hyperextend the elbows

## Common Mistakes

### Using Momentum

Leaning back or swinging the torso to generate momentum. The preacher bench is designed to prevent this; if you are generating momentum, the weight is too heavy.

### Partial Range of Motion

Not lowering to full extension (lost stretch) or not curling to full contraction (lost peak). Full range of motion is essential for complete biceps development.

### Hyperextending the Elbows

Locking the elbows aggressively at the bottom of each rep stresses the elbow joint. Stop just short of full lockout with the arms extended.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★☆☆☆☆.** The Preacher Curl has no direct transfer to running performance. It is included for arm development as part of the programme's physique goals, and its strict, low-fatigue format fits the concurrent training structure well.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Dumbbell Curl** | Greater freedom of movement; more stabiliser demand; easier to cheat |
| **Hammer Curl** | Neutral grip; emphasises brachioradialis; complements the Preacher Curl |
| **Cable Curl** | Constant tension throughout the range |

<CoachNote>
The Preacher Curl is a "feel" exercise; the weight matters less than the quality of contraction and the stretch at the bottom. If you cannot feel your biceps working on every rep, reduce the weight. Biceps respond well to high-quality volume with strict form, not heavy cheating reps.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
Long-length partial training and exercises that load the biceps at a stretched position have been shown to produce comparable or superior hypertrophy to shorter-range alternatives in some studies. The preacher bench's fixed path allows the biceps to be loaded near full extension, a position where the muscle is under significant tension.
</ScientificNote>
