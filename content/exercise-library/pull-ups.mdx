Pull-Ups are the highest-return upper body exercise available. They develop the lats, biceps, forearms, and grip while demanding core stability. Bodyweight loading provides automatic intensity regulation — as bodyweight changes or strength increases, the stimulus adjusts accordingly.

## Purpose

Latissimus dorsi and biceps hypertrophy and strength. Vertical pulling strength, grip development, and core stability.

## Muscles

| Role | Muscles |
|------|---------|
| **Primary** | Latissimus dorsi, biceps brachii |
| **Secondary** | Forearm flexors (grip), trapezius, rhomboids, posterior deltoid, core stabilisers |

## Execution

1. Grip the bar with palms facing away (pronated grip), hands slightly wider than shoulder-width
2. Hang with arms fully extended, shoulders engaged (not completely relaxed)
3. Pull yourself up by driving the elbows down and back until the chin clears the bar
4. Lower under control to full arm extension

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 3 × As many as possible (AMAP) |
| **Grip** | Pronated, slightly wider than shoulder-width |
| **Range** | Full — dead hang to chin over bar |
| **Tempo** | Controlled; avoid kipping or using momentum |

<CoachNote>
If you cannot yet perform full pull-ups, use the assisted pull-up machine or lat pulldown as a progression. The goal is to build toward unassisted pull-ups. Even a single full pull-up is a meaningful achievement — and a foundation to build on. Do not avoid the exercise because you cannot do many reps. Start where you are.
</CoachNote>

## Transfer Ratings

| Domain | Rating | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Running** | ★★☆☆☆ | Indirect — upper back strength supports running posture, especially during fatigue |
| **Strength** | ★★★★☆ | Excellent upper body strength developer; high loading relative to bodyweight |
| **Athleticism** | ★★★★☆ | Bodyweight control, grip strength, and upper body power transfer to climbing, throwing, and grappling |

## Common Mistakes

### Partial Reps

Not lowering to full extension (skipping the bottom) or not clearing the chin (skipping the top). Full range of motion is essential for complete lat and biceps development.

### Kipping

Using hip momentum to generate upward movement rather than pulling with the lats and arms. Kipping turns a strength exercise into a momentum exercise. Each rep should be a controlled pull.

### Neglecting the Eccentric

Dropping from the top rather than lowering under control. The eccentric phase is highly hypertrophic — a 2–3 second lowering significantly increases the stimulus per rep.

## Progression

| Stage | Method |
|-------|--------|
| **Cannot do 1 rep** | Assisted machine, lat pulldown, or eccentric-only (jump to top, lower slowly) |
| **1–3 reps** | Multiple sets of low reps; accumulate volume across sets |
| **4–8 reps** | Standard programming; aim to add 1 rep per week |
| **8+ reps** | Consider adding weight (weighted pull-ups) if rep quality remains high |

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Lat Pulldown** | Machine equivalent; easier to adjust load and volume; good for building toward pull-ups |
| **Assisted Pull-Up Machine** | Counterweight assistance; direct transfer to full pull-ups |
| **Chin-Ups** | Supinated (palms facing you) grip; slightly greater biceps contribution |
