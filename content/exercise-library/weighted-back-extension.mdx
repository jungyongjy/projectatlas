## Overview

The Weighted Back Extension is performed on the 45° back extension bench and develops the spinal erectors, glutes, and hamstrings in a loaded hip hinge with built-in spinal support. It complements the Romanian Deadlift and Barbell Hip Thrust by adding a low-fatigue posterior chain movement with a focus on spinal erector strength and posture.

## Purpose

Spinal erector, glute, and hamstring strength. Posterior chain development and postural support through a supported hip hinge pattern, with lower spinal loading than free-weight alternatives.

## Execution

1. Position the pads of the back extension bench so the hip joint sits above the pad, allowing the torso to hinge freely
2. Hook the feet under the ankle supports
3. Start with the torso in line with the legs, hands holding a plate across the chest (or at the sides for bodyweight)
4. Hinge forward by flexing at the hips, maintaining a neutral spine, until the torso is roughly parallel to the floor
5. Drive back to the starting position by extending the hips, squeezing the glutes at the top
6. Stop at neutral; do not hyperextend the spine past the line of the legs

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 2 × 8 |
| **Load** | Plate held across the chest; add weight once 8 controlled reps are easy |
| **Range** | Hips flexed to torso parallel, back to neutral (no hyperextension) |
| **Tempo** | Controlled; 2-second eccentric, pause at the top |

## Common Mistakes

### Hyperextending at the Top

Arching the lower back beyond the line of the legs at the top of the movement. The erectors should bring the torso to neutral, not beyond it. Stop at the point where the body forms a straight line.

### Rounding the Lower Back

Letting the lumbar spine round as you hinge forward. Maintain a neutral spine by keeping the chest up and the shoulders back throughout the range.

### Using Momentum

Swinging up through the hips rather than extending with the posterior chain. Control both the descent and the return.

## Running Transfer

**Transfer: ★★★☆☆.** Spinal erector and posterior chain strength support upright running posture and help delay the form breakdown that occurs with fatigue. The supported hip hinge trains the posterior chain through a large range of motion with minimal systemic fatigue, a favourable trade-off for a concurrent training programme.

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Romanian Deadlift** | Free-weight hip hinge with heavier loading and more hamstring emphasis |
| **Good Morning** | Barbell on the back; higher spinal loading and technical demand |
| **Hip Thrust** | Glute-dominant hip extension; complements the Back Extension |

<CoachNote>
The Back Extension is easy to rush and easy to overreach. The quality that matters is the pause at the top and the controlled, neutral-spine descent. A plate across the chest provides ample load for most athletes; resist the urge to load it like a deadlift. This is a low-fatigue support movement, not a max-strength lift.
</CoachNote>

<ScientificNote>
Back extension training strengthens the spinal erectors and posterior chain with lower compressive spinal loading than axial-loaded exercises such as the deadlift or good morning. For an athlete whose recovery is shared between strength and running, the supported hinge pattern delivers meaningful posterior chain work while sparing the spine.
</ScientificNote>
