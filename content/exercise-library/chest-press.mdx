The Machine Chest Press provides horizontal pressing for chest, anterior deltoid, and triceps development in a controlled movement path. It replaces the Barbell Bench Press — which the athlete dislikes and which carries higher injury risk without proportionate benefit for a non-powerlifting programme.

## Purpose

Chest, anterior deltoid, and triceps hypertrophy. Upper body pressing strength without the technical demands or injury risk of barbell bench pressing.

## Muscles

| Role | Muscles |
|------|---------|
| **Primary** | Pectoralis major, anterior deltoid, triceps brachii |
| **Secondary** | Serratus anterior (stabilising scapula) |

## Execution

1. Adjust the seat so the handles align with mid-chest level
2. Grip the handles with a comfortable width
3. Press forward until arms are extended but not locked out
4. Control the return to the starting position

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Sets × Reps** | 3 × 8–12 |
| **Range** | Full — handles to chest level at bottom, near-full extension at top |
| **Tempo** | Controlled; 2-second eccentric |

## Transfer Ratings

| Domain | Rating | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Running** | ★☆☆☆☆ | Minimal direct transfer; chest strength is not a performance factor in running |
| **Strength** | ★★★☆☆ | Moderate; machine path limits stabiliser contribution |
| **Athleticism** | ★★☆☆☆ | Indirect; horizontal pressing supports pushing movements in daily life |

## Common Mistakes

### Short Range of Motion

Not bringing the handles back far enough (lost stretch) or not extending enough (lost contraction). Use the full available range of the machine.

### Unilateral Pressing

One arm pressing harder than the other, creating uneven development. Focus on pressing evenly with both arms — the machine should move symmetrically.

<CoachNote>
The Chest Press is in the programme for upper body muscular development — a physique goal, not a performance goal. It is safe, efficient, and well-tolerated. For an athlete who dislikes barbell bench pressing, this exercise provides comparable chest stimulus with better adherence and lower injury risk.
</CoachNote>

## Alternatives

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Barbell Bench Press** | Greater stabiliser demand and loading potential; higher injury risk and technical demand |
| **Dumbbell Press** | Unilateral loading exposes asymmetries; greater range of motion; requires more setup |
| **Push-Ups** | Bodyweight alternative; high rep ranges for hypertrophy; easy to perform anywhere |
