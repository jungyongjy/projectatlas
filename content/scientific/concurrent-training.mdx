Concurrent training (combining strength and endurance exercise) produces unique physiological interactions. Understanding these interactions allows programming that minimises interference while maximising the benefits of both modalities.

## The Interference Effect: What the Evidence Shows

The interference effect is real but its magnitude is often overstated. Early research (Hickson, 1980) demonstrated that combining strength and endurance training reduced strength development compared to strength training alone. Subsequent research has clarified several important nuances:

### What We Know

- **Strength development is more affected than endurance development.** Endurance training interferes with strength gains more than the reverse.
- **The effect is modest, not dramatic.** Most studies show attenuated (not eliminated) strength gains. Concurrent trainees still get stronger; they just gain strength more slowly than those training strength exclusively.
- **Volume and frequency matter more than modality.** The interference effect scales with training volume. Moderate volumes of both modalities produce minimal interference.
- **Scheduling matters.** Separating strength and endurance sessions by at least 6–24 hours reduces interference compared to same-day training.

### Molecular Mechanisms (Simplified)

| Pathway | Activated By | Primary Effect |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| **mTOR** | Resistance training, protein intake | Muscle protein synthesis, hypertrophy |
| **AMPK** | Endurance training, energy depletion | Mitochondrial biogenesis, oxidative adaptation |

These pathways can inhibit each other when simultaneously activated. However, this molecular interference is less significant than the practical interference created by cumulative fatigue, tired muscles cannot produce maximal force, regardless of molecular signalling.

<ScientificNote>
The molecular interference hypothesis (AMPK inhibiting mTOR) was elegantly demonstrated in cell culture and animal models but has proven more nuanced in human training studies. The practical interference of residual fatigue (being too tired from running to lift with sufficient intensity) appears to be a more significant factor in real-world concurrent training outcomes than molecular-level signalling conflicts.
</ScientificNote>

## Programming Implications for Atlas

Based on the evidence, Atlas applies these principles:

1. **Schedule separation**: Hard runs and hard gym sessions are separated by at least one sleep cycle
2. **Volume moderation**: Neither modality is trained at maximal volume (3 runs, 3 gym sessions)
3. **Running priority**: When recovery is limited, running quality is preserved over gym intensity
4. **Protein adequacy**: 140–160 g/day supports both strength and endurance adaptation
5. **Periodised deloads**: Every 8–10 weeks to clear accumulated fatigue from both modalities
