Recovery is not what happens between sessions; it is the process by which training stimulus becomes adaptation. Training provides the signal; recovery provides the response. Neither is sufficient alone.

## Sleep: The Most Powerful Recovery Tool

Sleep is the single most important recovery variable, more impactful than any supplement, modality, or nutrition strategy.

| Sleep Duration | Effect on Athletic Performance |
|---------------|-------------------------------|
| 8+ hours | Optimal recovery; supports MPS, hormone production, and cognitive function |
| 7 hours | Adequate for most; slightly reduced recovery capacity |
| 6 hours | Measurable performance and recovery impairment |
| Under 5 hours | Significant impairment across all performance and recovery markers |

<ScientificNote>
Sleep deprivation reduces growth hormone secretion (which peaks during deep sleep), impairs glycogen replenishment, increases cortisol (catabolic), and reduces testosterone (anabolic). Sleep extension studies (where athletes increase sleep duration to 9–10 hours) consistently show improvements in performance, reaction time, and subjective well-being. The evidence strongly suggests that most athletes would benefit from sleeping more, not from any supplement or recovery modality.
</ScientificNote>

## Nutrition and Recovery

| Factor | Evidence Summary |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Protein timing** | Modest benefit from consuming protein within 1–2 hours post-training; total daily intake dominates |
| **Carbohydrate timing** | Relevant when training twice daily with under 8 hours between sessions; less critical for once-daily training |
| **Creatine** | Modestly supports recovery through enhanced phosphocreatine resynthesis and reduced muscle damage markers |
| **Omega-3 fatty acids** | May modestly reduce inflammation; evidence is mixed |
| **Antioxidant supplements** | May impair adaptation by blunting the inflammatory signal required for training adaptation; whole food sources preferred |

## Recovery Modalities: What Works?

| Modality | Evidence | Atlas Recommendation |
|----------|---------|---------------------|
| **Sleep** | Strong: the dominant recovery variable | 7–8 hours minimum |
| **Nutrition** | Strong: protein and energy balance essential | 140–160 g protein, adequate calories |
| **Active recovery** | Weak-to-moderate: small benefit over passive rest | Optional; gentle movement on rest days |
| **Massage/foam rolling** | Weak: temporary symptom relief, no adaptation benefit | Optional post-training; not a recovery requirement |
| **Cold water immersion** | Mixed: may reduce soreness but may also blunt hypertrophy adaptation | Not recommended for regular use |
| **Compression garments** | Weak: small effect on perceived recovery | Not recommended |
| **Stretching** | No effect on recovery or injury prevention | Post-training if desired; not a recovery tool |
