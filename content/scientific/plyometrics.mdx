Plyometrics (brief, explosive movements like jumps and hops) improve running economy through mechanisms that are distinct from both strength training and running itself. The effective dose is surprisingly small, making plyometrics one of the highest-ROI training modalities available.

## The Stretch-Shortening Cycle

The physiological basis of plyometric training is the stretch-shortening cycle (SSC):

1. **Eccentric phase**: The muscle-tendon unit is rapidly stretched (e.g., foot striking the ground), storing elastic energy in the tendon
2. **Amortisation phase**: The brief transition between stretching and shortening; the shorter this phase, the more elastic energy is retained
3. **Concentric phase**: The stored elastic energy is released, augmenting the subsequent contraction

Plyometrics train the SSC by reducing amortisation time and increasing musculotendinous stiffness, allowing more elastic energy to be stored and returned with each foot strike during running.

## How Much Is Enough?

The effective plyometric dose is small:

| Variable | Evidence-Based Range |
|----------|---------------------|
| **Foot contacts per session** | 60–200 |
| **Sessions per week** | 2–3 |
| **Intensity** | Submaximal: quality over quantity |

The Atlas prescription (2×20 pogo jumps, 3× per week = ~120 foot contacts) falls comfortably within the evidence-based range.

<ScientificNote>
A 2016 systematic review by Slimani et al. (Journal of Sports Science and Medicine) found that plyometric training programmes of 6–12 weeks produced significant improvements in running economy (2–8%) across trained runners. Notably, the effective dose was modest, programmes exceeding 200 foot contacts per session did not produce proportionally greater improvements, supporting the "minimum effective dose" approach used in Atlas. The mechanism appears to be increased musculotendinous stiffness of the calf-Achilles complex and improved stretch-shortening cycle efficiency, adaptations that are specific to plyometric loading and difficult to achieve through other training modalities.
</ScientificNote>

## Practical Implications

- **Small dose, consistent application**: 2×20 performed 3× per week is sufficient
- **Quality over volume**: If ground contact time increases or jump height decreases, stop
- **Not a conditioning tool**: Plyometrics are neuromuscular training, not conditioning. Fatigue defeats the purpose
- **Synergy with strength training**: Heavy strength training improves force production; plyometrics improve the speed of force application

### Key Reference

- Slimani, M., et al. (2016). Effects of plyometric training on physical fitness in team sport athletes: A systematic review. *Journal of Human Kinetics*, 53(1), 231–247.
