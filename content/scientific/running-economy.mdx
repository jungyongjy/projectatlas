Running economy is the energy cost of running at a given speed. Two runners with identical VO₂max values can have substantially different race times, the difference is often economy. Improving running economy means running faster without becoming fitter.

## Determinants of Running Economy

| Factor | Contribution | Modifiable? |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| **Musculotendinous stiffness** | Major | Yes: plyometrics, heavy strength training |
| **Ground contact time** | Significant | Yes: cadence work, plyometrics |
| **Vertical oscillation** | Moderate | Partially: technique work |
| **Limb length and proportion** | Minor | No: structural |
| **Muscle fibre type** | Moderate | Partially: training influences fibre characteristics |
| **Anthropometry** | Minor | Partially: body composition affects energy cost |

## How Training Improves Running Economy

### Strength Training

Heavy resistance training improves running economy by 2–8% in trained runners. The mechanism is primarily increased musculotendinous stiffness and improved force production capacity, each foot strike produces more forward propulsion with less energy.

Key findings:
- Both heavy strength training (3–5 RM) and explosive strength training improve economy
- The effect is present after 6–14 weeks of consistent training
- Concurrent strength and endurance training does not diminish the economy benefit

### Plyometrics

Plyometric training improves running economy by 2–8%, similar in magnitude to heavy strength training, but through a different mechanism: enhanced stretch-shortening cycle function. The combination of heavy strength (force production) and plyometrics (force application speed) produces superior results.

### Altitude/Hypoxic Training

Not relevant to the Atlas programme (no altitude access). Included for completeness, improves economy through haematological adaptations that are difficult to replicate at sea level.

<ScientificNote>
A 2016 meta-analysis by Barnes & Kilding (Sports Medicine) examined 51 studies on running economy interventions. Strength training produced the most consistent and largest improvements (2–8%), followed by plyometrics (2–7%). Altitude exposure and heat training showed more variable results. Notably, the combination of heavy strength training and plyometrics produced greater improvements than either alone, supporting the Atlas approach of including both modalities.
</ScientificNote>

## Practical Implications for Atlas

- **Strength training is non-negotiable**: It improves running economy independently of aerobic fitness
- **Plyometrics earn their place**: 2×20 pogo jumps/ankle hops, performed consistently, contribute to economy improvements
- **Technique matters**: Cadence, posture, and relaxation are trainable economy factors
- **Body composition is relevant**: Excess body mass increases energy cost. The Atlas physique target (~80–84 kg, ~12% body fat) balances muscularity with running efficiency
