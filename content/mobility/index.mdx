Mobility in Atlas is treated as **maintenance**, not a separate training goal. The objective is to maintain sufficient range of motion for quality training, reduce unnecessary stiffness, and address specific limitations that affect running or lifting performance.

## Philosophy

Atlas takes a targeted approach to mobility:

- **Maintain what is good** — Deep squat, toe touch, overhead squat, cross-legged sitting are already strong. Preserve them with minimal ongoing work.
- **Improve what is limited** — Thoracic mobility, upper back tightness, and calf tightness are the priority areas.
- **Support training** — Mobility work exists to improve running and lifting quality, not to achieve flexibility for its own sake.

<InfoBox title="Mobility is not stretching">
Mobility is the ability to move through a range of motion with control. Flexibility is passive range of motion. Mobility requires both range and strength throughout that range — you need to be able to access a position and own it.
</InfoBox>

## The 5–10 Minute Daily Minimum

Mobility work is intentionally brief. Five to ten minutes daily is sufficient to maintain joint health and address limitations. The priority order:

| Priority | Area | Why |
|----------|------|-----|
| **1** | Thoracic Spine | Primary limitation; affects posture, breathing, overhead movement |
| **2** | Calves | Running-related tightness; affects ankle mobility and gait |
| **3** | Ankles | Running mechanics; dorsiflexion affects stride and force absorption |
| **4** | Hips | Maintain current good function; support squatting and hinging |
| **5** | Shoulders | Maintain overhead range; support pressing and pulling |

<CoachNote>
Five minutes of mobility work performed daily produces far greater annual volume than 30 minutes performed once per week. Consistency matters more than duration. Attach mobility to an existing habit — after brushing teeth, before showering, while watching TV. The trigger is more important than the duration.
</CoachNote>
