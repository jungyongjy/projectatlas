The pre-training warm-up serves one purpose: prepare the body for the work ahead. It is not a mobility session in itself; it is the minimum necessary to train safely and effectively.

<Figure name="warmUpRoutine" caption="Warm-up sequence: raise body temperature, mobilise, then activate" />

## General Principles

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|------------|
| **Dynamic, not static** | Move through ranges, do not hold stretches |
| **Specific** | Prime the movements you are about to perform |
| **Brief** | 5 minutes maximum |
| **Progressive** | Start small, build to full range and speed |

<InfoBox title="Static stretching before training">
Static stretching (holding a stretch for 30+ seconds) before training reduces power output by 5–10% for up to 30 minutes and does not reduce injury risk. Save static stretching for after training or rest days. Pre-training mobility is dynamic, moving through ranges without prolonged holds.
</InfoBox>

## Gym Warm-Up (3–5 minutes)

### Phase 1: General Movement (1–2 minutes)
- Light jogging on the spot or walking for 60 seconds
- Arm circles (forward and backward, 10 each)
- Bodyweight squats (10 reps, full range)

### Phase 2: Dynamic Mobility (1–2 minutes)
- Thoracic rotations (5 per side)
- Leg swings, forward/backward (10 per leg)
- Leg swings, lateral (10 per leg)
- Hip circles (5 each direction)

### Phase 3: Specific Priming (1 minute)
Before your first exercise, perform 1–2 warm-up sets at 50% and 75% of working weight. This primes the specific movement pattern and prepares the nervous system.

<CoachNote>
The warm-up sets on your first exercise are the most important part of the gym warm-up. The general movement and mobility work get you ready to move; the warm-up sets get you ready to lift. Do not skip them to save time.
</CoachNote>

## Running Warm-Up (5–8 minutes)

This is the same warm-up documented in the Running Warm-Up page. See that page for the complete protocol.

### Quick Reference
1. **Light jogging** (2–3 minutes)
2. **Leg swings**: forward and lateral
3. **High knees**: hip flexor activation, cadence patterning
4. **Butt kicks**: hamstring activation
5. **Dynamic hamstring swings**
6. **Ankle rocks**: ankle preparation
7. **Walking lunges**: hip mobility under load
8. **Calf raises** (15–20 bodyweight reps)

For hard run sessions, add 2–3 × 50 m primer strides after the warm-up.

## What to Avoid

| Don't | Because |
|-------|---------|
| Static stretching | Reduces power; does not prevent injury |
| Foam rolling extensively | Better placed post-training or on rest days |
| Skipping the warm-up entirely | Cold tissues at load = heightened injury risk |
| Excessive warm-up | Fatigue before the session begins defeats the purpose |

<TipBox>
If you are short on time, do a shorter warm-up, not no warm-up. Even 90 seconds of dynamic movement (leg swings, bodyweight squats, arm circles) is far better than starting cold.
</TipBox>

## Weather Adjustments

| Condition | Adjustment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Cold** (under 15°C) | Extend general movement phase by 2–3 minutes |
| **Hot** (over 30°C) | Standard warm-up; body temperature is already elevated |
| **Early morning** | Warm-up may need to be slightly longer: core temperature is lowest upon waking |
| **Indoors (gym)** | Standard warm-up; air-conditioned environment is consistent |
