Hip mobility is a current strength for this athlete; deep squat, toe touch, and overhead squat are all comfortable positions. The objective is **maintenance**, not dramatic improvement. Preserve what works.

## Current Status

| Movement | Status | Priority |
|----------|--------|----------|
| Deep squat | Comfortable | Maintain |
| Toe touch | Full range | Maintain |
| Overhead squat | Comfortable | Maintain |
| Cross-legged sitting | Comfortable | Maintain |
| Hip rotation | Adequate | Maintain |

<InfoBox title="If it is not broken, maintain it">
The hips are the mobility success story. Unlike the thoracic spine and calves (which need focused improvement), the hips need only enough attention to preserve current function. This is a much smaller time investment.
</InfoBox>

## The Maintenance Routine

Total time: approximately 2–3 minutes. Perform 3–4 times per week or after prolonged sitting.

<Figure name="hip9090" caption="Hip 90/90 stretch position with both hips flexed to 90 degrees" />

<DrillCard title="1. 90/90 Hip Stretch">
  <DrillStep term="Position">Seated, front leg bent to 90°, back leg bent to 90°</DrillStep>
  <DrillStep term="Movement">Sit tall; optionally lean forward over front shin</DrillStep>
  <DrillStep term="Hold">30–45 seconds per side</DrillStep>
</DrillCard>

This position maintains both internal and external hip rotation simultaneously.

<DrillCard title="2. Hip Flexor Stretch (Half-Kneeling)">
  <DrillStep term="Position">Half-kneeling, back knee down, front foot planted</DrillStep>
  <DrillStep term="Movement">Tuck pelvis posteriorly (flatten the lower back) and shift forward</DrillStep>
  <DrillStep term="Hold">30–45 seconds per side</DrillStep>
</DrillCard>

Desk work shortens hip flexors. This stretch counters that adaptation and maintains hip extension range for running.

<DrillCard title="3. Deep Squat Hold">
  <DrillStep term="Position">Feet flat, heels down, squat as deep as comfortable</DrillStep>
  <DrillStep term="Movement">Hold the bottom position; use elbows to gently push knees outward</DrillStep>
  <DrillStep term="Hold">30–60 seconds</DrillStep>
</DrillCard>

The deep squat is already comfortable. Holding it regularly maintains the range. This is a "use it or lose it" capacity.

<DrillCard title="4. Couch Stretch (Optional)">
  <DrillStep term="Position">Half-kneeling with back foot elevated on a wall or couch</DrillStep>
  <DrillStep term="Movement">Tuck pelvis and shift forward to feel stretch in quad and hip flexor</DrillStep>
  <DrillStep term="Hold">30–45 seconds per side</DrillStep>
</DrillCard>

Include only if hip flexor tightness is felt during running or squatting. Otherwise, the half-kneeling stretch above is sufficient.

<CoachNote>
Hip mobility maintenance is a low-effort investment with high long-term returns. The positions you do not use, you lose. Spending 2–3 minutes in a deep squat and 90/90 position a few times per week is almost certainly sufficient to preserve current hip function for decades.
</CoachNote>

## Common Mistakes

### Overworking the Hips

The hips already have good mobility. Spending significant time "improving" them is inefficient; that time is better allocated to thoracic mobility and calf work. Maintenance, not optimisation.

### Aggressive Stretching

Hips that already have full range do not benefit from aggressive stretching. Gentle, comfortable end-range holds are sufficient. Pain is never the goal.

### Neglecting the Hips Entirely

The opposite error: because hips are currently good, neglecting them entirely. Mobility is "use it or lose it." A few minutes per week of maintenance prevents the slow decline that desk work and ageing would otherwise produce.

<ScientificNote>
Hip joint health is strongly correlated with longevity and quality of life in ageing populations. Hip mobility in early adulthood (20s–30s) predicts hip function in later decades. The work done now (even the minimal maintenance described here) has compounding returns over a lifetime.
</ScientificNote>
