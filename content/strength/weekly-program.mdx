The strength programme uses two rotating full-body templates performed three times per week. This structure maximises exercise frequency while maintaining manageable session duration.

## The Rotation

| Week | Monday | Wednesday | Saturday |
|------|--------|-----------|----------|
| Week 1 | A | B | A |
| Week 2 | B | A | B |
| Week 3 | A | B | A |

This ensures each template is performed 4–5 times per month — adequate frequency for strength and hypertrophy maintenance while providing sufficient variation.

## Session Structure

Every gym session follows the same structure:

1. **Warm-Up** — 2–3 minutes dynamic movement; warm-up sets for first exercise
2. **Main Work** — Superset pairs performed with ~60 seconds rest between sets
3. **Accessory Work** — Isolation movements at end of session
4. **Plyometrics** — Brief, low-volume plyometric work after calf raises

<InfoBox title="Supersets explained">
A superset pairs two exercises performed back-to-back with minimal rest between them. You rest after completing both exercises. This cuts session time nearly in half without compromising quality — the rest period for one muscle group becomes the work period for another.
</InfoBox>

<CoachNote>
The order of exercises within each template is intentional. Compound movements come first (when you are fresh). Isolation work follows. Core work at the end ensures your trunk is not pre-fatigued for compound lifts that require stability.
</CoachNote>

## Execution Guidelines

### Rest Periods
- **Supersets**: Rest ~60 seconds after completing both exercises
- **Straight sets** (if not supersetting): Rest 60–90 seconds

### Tempo
- **Concentric** (lifting): Controlled, explosive intent
- **Eccentric** (lowering): 2–3 seconds, controlled

### Range of Motion
- Full range of motion on every exercise unless a specific limitation prevents it
- Partial reps are not programmed — they reduce stimulus without reducing fatigue

### Warm-Up Sets
- First exercise of each session: 1–2 light warm-up sets before working sets
- Subsequent exercises: No additional warm-up needed (muscles are already warm)

<TipBox>
If you have extra time, do not add exercises — improve the quality of the ones already programmed. Slower eccentrics, better mind-muscle connection, and more precise execution produce better results than additional volume performed with mediocre quality.
</TipBox>
