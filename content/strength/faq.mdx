## Programme Structure

### Why only three gym sessions per week?

Three full-body sessions provide adequate frequency for strength and hypertrophy maintenance while preserving recovery capacity for running. Four sessions would reduce running quality. Two sessions would provide insufficient stimulus. Three is the balance point.

### Can I add a fourth gym session?

No. The current structure is at the ceiling of recoverable volume given concurrent running demands. Adding a fourth session would compromise either running quality or recovery — and likely both.

### Why full-body instead of upper/lower split?

Full-body training provides higher frequency per muscle group (3× per week) than an upper/lower split (1–2× per week). For a programme with only three weekly sessions, full-body is more efficient. An upper/lower split would require four sessions for equivalent frequency.

### Why supersets?

Supersets reduce session time by approximately 40–50% without compromising quality. The rest period for one muscle group becomes the work period for another. In a 30-minute session constraint, supersets are not optional — they are essential.

## Exercise Questions

### Can I substitute exercises?

Generally, no. Each exercise was selected against specific criteria (stimulus, fatigue, safety, practicality, transfer). Substitutions should only be considered when:

- An injury prevents execution
- Equipment is unavailable
- The exercise consistently causes discomfort

If substitution is necessary, choose an exercise that satisfies the same criteria and serves the same role in the programme.

<CoachNote>
Before substituting an exercise because it "does not feel right," invest 2–3 sessions in improving technique. Many exercises feel awkward initially because they expose weaknesses — and those weaknesses are precisely why the exercise is in the programme.
</CoachNote>

### What if I cannot do pull-ups?

Use the assisted pull-up machine or lat pulldown as a progression toward full pull-ups. The goal is to build toward unassisted pull-ups — they are one of the highest-ROI exercises available.

### Why no barbell bench press?

The athlete dislikes it. A machine chest press provides comparable hypertrophy with lower technical demand, lower injury risk, and greater enjoyment. The theoretical advantages of barbell bench press (slightly greater stabiliser activation) do not outweigh the practical disadvantages for this athlete.

### Why no barbell squats?

Same rationale: the athlete dislikes them, they create disproportionate fatigue, they require significant technical investment, and they carry higher injury risk. Leg Press and Bulgarian Split Squats together provide comprehensive leg development without these drawbacks.

## Progression

### How do I know when to increase weight?

When you can complete the top of the prescribed rep range (e.g., 12 reps) with approximately RPE 8 — meaning you could have performed 2 more reps with good form. This ensures each weight increase is earned.

### What if I cannot complete the minimum reps?

Reduce weight slightly (5–10%) and work back up. If the problem persists across multiple sessions, check recovery fundamentals: sleep, protein, calories, stress.

### How fast should I progress?

At the intermediate level, expect to add weight every 2–4 weeks on most exercises. Progress is not linear. Some exercises will progress faster than others. Calves and lateral raises progress slowly — this is normal for isolation movements.

### Should I track my workouts?

Yes. Use Hevy or any notes app. Record exercise, weight, reps per set, and date. Without tracking, progression is guesswork.

## Recovery and Interference

### Should I train if I am sore?

Mild soreness: yes, train normally. Moderate soreness: train but consider reducing volume slightly. Severe soreness (painful to move through normal range): consider an extra rest day — severe soreness indicates the previous session exceeded recoverable volume.

### Does gym training affect my running the next day?

It can. Monday gym (with leg work) is followed by Tuesday Zone 2 — an easy run that is compatible with moderate leg fatigue. Wednesday gym is followed by Thursday rest. Saturday gym (with leg work) is followed by Sunday long run — also at easy effort.

The schedule is designed so that leg training never precedes a quality running session.

### What if I have to train gym and running on the same day?

Not ideal but survivable. Priority order:

1. Run first (running on fatigued legs reinforces poor mechanics)
2. Allow at least 4–6 hours between sessions if possible
3. Reduce gym volume if running quality was high
4. Do not make same-day training a habit — the schedule is designed to avoid it

## Troubleshooting

### I am not getting stronger

Check, in order:

1. **Consistency** — Have you completed 90%+ of scheduled sessions for 4+ weeks?
2. **Execution** — Are reps performed with full ROM and controlled tempo?
3. **Progression** — Are you attempting to progress (adding reps/weight) or just repeating?
4. **Recovery** — Sleep 7–8 hours? Protein 140–160 g/day? Calories adequate?
5. **Deload** — Has it been 8+ weeks since the last deload?

Most "programming problems" are actually recovery or execution problems.

### One side is stronger than the other

Unilateral exercises (Bulgarian Split Squats) will expose and eventually correct asymmetries. Always start with the weaker side and match reps on the stronger side. Do not do more reps on the stronger side — that widens the gap.

### I dread going to the gym

Distinguish between:

- **Low motivation** (normal, train anyway — motivation follows action)
- **Dreading specific exercises** (consider whether a substitution is appropriate)
- **Systemic fatigue** (may indicate need for a deload)
- **Burnout** (rare; may require programme adjustment or a mental break)

<CoachNote>
Motivation is unreliable. It comes and goes unpredictably. Discipline — doing the work regardless of how you feel — is the skill that produces results. Train when you are motivated. Train when you are not. The sessions you do not want to do are often the most important ones.
</CoachNote>
