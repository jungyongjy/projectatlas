The strength programme is designed around one organising principle: **strength supports running, not the reverse**. Every exercise, set, and rep scheme is evaluated against its effect on running performance and long-term sustainability.

## The Role of Strength

Strength training in Atlas serves five functions:

| Function | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------|
| **Running Economy** | Stronger muscles and tendons improve force return, reducing the energy cost of running |
| **Injury Prevention** | Strong tissues tolerate loading better; strength is the best injury insurance available |
| **Muscle Maintenance** | Preserving lean mass supports metabolic health, appearance, and resilience |
| **Force Production** | General strength underpins athleticism: sprinting, jumping, changing direction |
| **Joint Health** | Controlled loading through full ranges of motion maintains joint integrity |

<InfoBox title="Strength is not the performance metric">
Improving your squat, bench, or deadlift numbers is not a programme objective. If strength increases while running improves, excellent. If strength plateaus but running improves, the programme is working as designed.
</InfoBox>

## Design Principles

### Low Fatigue

Every exercise should produce high stimulus with minimal recovery cost. The programme deliberately avoids exercises that create disproportionate fatigue:

- No heavy barbell squats (high axial fatigue, long setup, high injury risk for this athlete)
- No conventional deadlifts (disproportionate lower back fatigue)
- No maximal-effort lifting (injury risk exceeds benefit for a non-strength athlete)

### High Efficiency

Thirty-minute sessions require ruthless efficiency. Every exercise must earn its place:

- Supersets minimise rest time without compromising quality
- Machine-dominant training reduces setup and technical demand
- Limited exercise selection, no filler movements
- Exercises that can be loaded progressively without complex technique

### Running Compatibility

Leg training is programmed to minimise interference with running:

- No leg-dominant session the day before a quality run
- Lower body volume is moderate, not maximal
- Exercise selection favours movements with low residual soreness

### Enjoyment

The programme must be sustainable. Exercises the athlete dislikes (even if theoretically optimal) are less valuable than exercises performed consistently:

- No barbell back squats (disliked)
- No conventional deadlifts (disliked)
- No heavy barbell bench press (disliked)
- Machine compounds and unilateral work preferred

<CoachNote>
Programming is not about what is "optimal" in the abstract. It is about what is optimal for a specific athlete given their goals, constraints, preferences, and training history. A programme you enjoy is a programme you follow. A programme you follow produces results. The rest is academic.
</CoachNote>

## Why Machines?

Atlas intentionally favours machine compounds over free-weight barbell lifts. This decision is based on practical considerations, not theoretical inferiority.

| Factor | Machine | Barbell |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Setup time** | Seconds | Minutes (loading, rack setup) |
| **Technical demand** | Low | High (form-dependent) |
| **Injury risk** | Lower | Higher (especially when fatigued) |
| **Fatigue cost** | Lower (no stabilisation demand) | Higher (full-body stabilisation) |
| **Progression** | Simple (pin-loaded increments) | Complex (plate math, micro-loading) |
| **Recovery** | Faster | Slower (greater systemic demand) |
| **Adherence** | Higher (less intimidating) | Lower (more demanding) |

<ScientificNote>
Research comparing machine-based and free-weight resistance training consistently finds comparable hypertrophy when volume is equated. Free weights may offer slightly greater stabiliser activation and sport-specific transfer, but these advantages are marginal for a non-strength athlete, and are outweighed by the practical benefits of machines for this user profile.
</ScientificNote>

## What the Programme Rejects

| Rejected | Reason |
|----------|--------|
| Bodybuilding splits | Too much volume, too little frequency, incompatible with running |
| Powerlifting programming | Excessive fatigue, injury risk, irrelevant to running goals |
| CrossFit-style circuits | Unpredictable fatigue, poor progression, high injury risk |
| "Functional fitness" | Undefined stimulus, difficult to progress, often fatiguing |
| Constant variation | Prevents progressive overload, novelty without adaptation |

<TipBox>
The programme stays the same so the results can change. If the programme changes every few weeks, you never know what is working. A stable programme produces clear feedback; you either got stronger or you did not.
</TipBox>
