Progression is the engine of adaptation. Without it, training becomes maintenance. But progression must be systematic, adding weight randomly or aggressively leads to plateaus, frustration, and injury.

Atlas uses **double progression**: the simplest reliable method for long-term strength development.

## What Is Double Progression?

Double progression has two stages:

1. **Rep progression**: Increase repetitions within a prescribed range at a fixed weight
2. **Load progression**: When the top of the rep range is reached with good form, increase the weight and drop back to the bottom of the rep range

### Example

| Week | Weight | Reps | Stage |
|------|--------|------|-------|
| 1 | 80 kg | 8 | Start at bottom of range |
| 2 | 80 kg | 9 | Rep progression |
| 3 | 80 kg | 10 | Rep progression |
| 4 | 85 kg | 8 | Load progression: weight increases, reps reset |

Repeat this cycle indefinitely.

<InfoBox title="Why this works">
Double progression ensures you "earn" each weight increase. You do not add load until you have demonstrated mastery at the current load. This prevents premature weight increases that degrade form and increase injury risk.
</InfoBox>

## Rep Ranges

Different rep ranges target different adaptations. Atlas uses three ranges.

| Range | Primary Adaptation | Used For |
|-------|-------------------|----------|
| **8–12** | Hypertrophy + strength | Most compound and isolation exercises |
| **10–15** | Hypertrophy + metabolic stress | Isolation work, rear delts, hip work |
| **12–20** | Endurance + hypertrophy | Calves, some isolation work |

## RPE Guideline

Progression is gated by effort, not just numbers. The rule: **increase load when the prescribed rep range can be completed at approximately RPE 8**.

RPE 8 means you could have performed 2 more reps with good form but chose not to. It does not mean failure.

<WarningBox title="Do not train to failure">
Training to failure on compound exercises dramatically increases fatigue without proportionally increasing adaptation. Leave 1–2 reps in reserve on most sets. Occasional failure on isolation exercises (last set of lateral raises, for example) is acceptable, but not on compounds.
</WarningBox>

## Progression Rate

Progression is not linear. It slows over time.

| Training Age | Expected Progression |
|-------------|---------------------|
| **Beginner** (0–1 year) | Weekly or bi-weekly increases |
| **Intermediate** (1–3 years) | Every 2–4 weeks |
| **Advanced** (3+ years) | Every 4–8 weeks or longer |

This athlete is intermediate. Expect modest, consistent progress, not weekly PRs.

<CoachNote>
After 7+ years of training, adding 2.5 kg to a lift every month is excellent progress. Social media has distorted expectations of what "normal" strength progression looks like. An intermediate lifter adding 10–15 kg to a compound lift over a year is doing well.
</CoachNote>

## What to Do When Progress Stalls

Stalling is normal. The response determines whether it becomes a plateau.

### Step 1: Verify the basics
- Sleep: 7–8 hours consistently?
- Protein: 140–160 g/day consistently?
- Calories: Adequate intake?
- Stress: Managed?

### Step 2: Check execution
- Are reps performed with full range of motion?
- Has tempo degraded (rushing through reps)?
- Are warm-up sets adequate?

### Step 3: Adjust programming
- **Option A**: Extend the rep range (e.g., 8–12 → 8–15) before increasing weight
- **Option B**: Reduce weight by 5–10% and rebuild with perfect form
- **Option C**: If multiple exercises have stalled, a deload may be needed

<ScientificNote>
Strength plateaus in intermediate lifters are typically caused by inadequate recovery rather than inadequate stimulus. Before adding volume or intensity, verify that recovery fundamentals (sleep, nutrition, stress management) are in place. Adding training stress to an under-recovered athlete worsens the problem.
</ScientificNote>

## Tracking

Progression requires tracking. Memory is unreliable.

### What to track
- Exercise, weight, reps per set, date
- Any notes on form, pain, or unusual fatigue

### How to track
- Hevy (primary workout logger)
- Any notes app
- The method matters less than the consistency

<TipBox>
The best tracking system is the one you use every session. A simple notes file updated consistently is more valuable than a sophisticated app you forget to open.
</TipBox>
