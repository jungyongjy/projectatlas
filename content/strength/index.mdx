The strength programme exists to support running performance, maintain muscle mass, and build a resilient, athletic body. It is not a powerlifting programme, a bodybuilding split, or a general fitness routine.

## What You'll Find Here

- **Philosophy** — Why strength training is structured the way it is
- **Concurrent Training** — How to train for strength and endurance simultaneously
- **Weekly Program** — The rotating A/B full-body templates
- **Progression Model** — Double progression explained
- **Exercise Selection** — Why each exercise earned its place
- **Deload Strategy** — When and how to reduce fatigue
- **FAQ** — Common questions answered

<InfoBox title="Strength is support, not the goal">
The primary performance metric is the 2.4 km run. Strength training improves running economy, reduces injury risk, and maintains muscle — but improving your one-rep max is not a programme objective. Every strength decision is evaluated against its effect on running.
</InfoBox>

## Programme at a Glance

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Frequency** | 3 sessions per week |
| **Duration** | ~30 minutes per session |
| **Style** | Full-body, supersets, machine-dominant |
| **Split** | Rotating A/B templates |
| **Progression** | Double progression (reps → load → reps) |
| **Rest** | ~60 seconds between sets |

<CoachNote>
Thirty minutes may not sound like much. But three 30-minute sessions per week, performed consistently for 52 weeks, produces 78 hours of quality strength work per year. Consistency amplifies modest effort into significant results.
</CoachNote>
