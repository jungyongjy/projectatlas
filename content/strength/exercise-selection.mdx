Every exercise in the Atlas programme has been selected against five criteria. Exercises that fail to satisfy these criteria are replaced. This page documents the rationale behind each selection.

## Selection Criteria

| Criterion | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| **High Stimulus** | Does it produce meaningful adaptation? |
| **Low Fatigue** | Is the recovery cost proportionate to the benefit? |
| **Easy Progression** | Can it be overloaded simply and reliably? |
| **Safe** | Is the injury risk acceptably low? |
| **Practical** | Is it available in a commercial gym? |

<InfoBox title="No exercise is permanent">
Exercise selection is reviewed periodically. If a better option exists (higher stimulus, lower fatigue, safer, more practical), the programme should change. But changes should be rare, deliberate, and evidence-informed, not impulsive.
</InfoBox>

## Compound Movements

### Leg Press
**Why it was selected**: Quad-dominant compound with minimal axial fatigue. Heavier loading than Bulgarian Split Squats without the balance demand. Easy to progress (pin-loaded or plate-loaded).

**What it replaced**: Barbell Back Squat (disliked, high fatigue, high technical demand, high injury risk for this athlete).

**Transfer**: Moderate running transfer through quad and glute strength. Low direct transfer compared to unilateral work.

### Machine Shoulder Press
**Why it was selected**: Stable overhead pressing without the technical demand of a barbell. Develops anterior and medial deltoids and triceps. Easy to progress with pin-loaded increments.

**Transfer**: Moderate. Shoulder strength supports arm drive during running but is not a primary performance driver.

### Chest Press
**Why it was selected**: Horizontal pressing for chest, anterior deltoid, and triceps development. Machine variant chosen for stability, safety, and ease of progression.

**What it replaced**: Barbell Bench Press (disliked, higher injury risk without proportionate benefit for a non-powerlifting programme).

**Transfer**: Low direct running transfer. Supports upper body muscularity and a balanced physique.

### Pull-Ups
**Why it was selected**: The highest-ROI vertical pull. Develops lats, biceps, grip, and core stability. Bodyweight provides automatic load regulation.

**Transfer**: Moderate. Upper back strength supports running posture. Grip strength transfers to daily life and other lifts.

### Seated Cable Row
**Why it was selected**: Horizontal pull for mid-back thickness and scapular control. Cable provides constant tension throughout the range of motion.

**Transfer**: Moderate. Scapular retraction and thoracic posture support running form, especially during fatigue.

### Bulgarian Split Squat
**Why it was selected**: Unilateral quad and glute development with hip stability and balance demands. Direct transfer to running through single-leg strength. Addresses unilateral weaknesses that bilateral exercises mask.

**What it replaced**: Leg Extensions (effective quad isolation but poor overall ROI, no stability demand, no glute contribution, no athletic transfer).

**Transfer**: High running transfer through unilateral stability, quad strength, and hip control.

### Romanian Deadlift
**Why it was selected**: Posterior chain development (hamstrings, glutes, erectors) with lower fatigue than conventional deadlifts. No floor pull and reduced lower back stress. Direct hamstring and glute hypertrophy.

**What it replaced**: Conventional Deadlift (high fatigue, high lower back stress, disliked by athlete).

**Transfer**: Moderate running transfer through posterior chain strength. Hamstring strength supports running propulsion.

<CoachNote>
The Romanian Deadlift is the most technically demanding exercise in the programme. Prioritise hamstring stretch and control over load. A perfectly executed set at a moderate weight produces better results than a heavy set with a rounded lumbar spine.
</CoachNote>

## Isolation and Arm Work

### Seated Leg Curl
**Why it was selected**: Hamstring isolation for the knee flexion role, complementing the Romanian Deadlift (hip extension). Minimal systemic fatigue with a high stimulus-to-fatigue ratio.

**Transfer**: Moderate. Hamstring strength supports running propulsion and eccentric knee control during gait.

### Rear Delt Reverse Fly
**Why it was selected**: Rear delt and upper back development. The machine provides a stable, controlled movement. Complements the Face Pull for complete posterior shoulder development.

**Transfer**: Indirect but important. Shoulder health enables consistent training; the movement counterbalances pressing and desk posture.

### Face Pull
**Why it was selected**: Rear delt and external rotator development. Critical for shoulder health and posture; counterbalances the internal rotation bias of pressing and desk work.

**Transfer**: Indirect but important. Shoulder health enables consistent training. Postural benefits transfer to daily life.

### Preacher Curl
**Why it was selected**: Biceps isolation with strict form enforced by the preacher bench. Eliminates momentum. High stimulus-to-fatigue ratio.

**Transfer**: Minimal direct running transfer. Maintains upper body muscularity, important for physique goals.

### Hammer Curl
**Why it was selected**: Brachioradialis and biceps development with a neutral grip (reduced wrist stress). Complements the Preacher Curl for complete arm development.

**Transfer**: Same rationale as the Preacher Curl.

### Cable Triceps Extension
**Why it was selected**: Triceps isolation with constant cable tension. High stimulus-to-fatigue ratio. Supports pressing strength and arm appearance. Included in both Full Body A and Full Body B for adequate weekly volume.

**Transfer**: Moderate. Triceps contribute to pressing movements. Arm appearance supports physique goals.

### Seated Lateral Raise
**Why it was selected**: Medial deltoid isolation, the only effective way to develop shoulder width. High stimulus-to-fatigue ratio. Included in both Full Body A and Full Body B for adequate frequency.

**Transfer**: Low direct running transfer. High physique transfer; shoulder width significantly affects upper body appearance.

<InfoBox title="Why direct arm work is retained">
Arms contribute significantly to overall physique. Removing direct arm work would provide minimal recovery benefit (arms recover quickly) while noticeably reducing hypertrophy stimulus. The ROI calculation favours retention, with approximately six direct sets each for biceps and triceps per week.
</InfoBox>

## Core and Posterior Chain

### Decline Crunch
**Why it was selected**: Rectus abdominis development through spinal flexion on a decline bench, loading the abs through a full range with adjustable resistance and minimal fatigue.

**Transfer**: Low direct running transfer, where the core's primary role is anti-rotation and force transfer. Included for balanced core development and physique.

### Pallof Press
**Why it was selected**: Anti-rotation core exercise. Develops the obliques and deep spinal stabilisers in a movement pattern that resists unwanted rotation, directly applicable to running.

**Transfer**: High. Resisting rotation while the limbs move is precisely what the core does during running.

### Weighted Back Extension
**Why it was selected**: Posterior chain and spinal erector development in a supported hip hinge, building posture and back strength with lower spinal loading than free-weight alternatives.

**Transfer**: Moderate. Spinal erector and posterior chain strength support upright running posture and delay fatigue-related form breakdown.

### Barbell Hip Thrust
**Why it was selected**: Direct glute isolation with high loading potential. Glute strength and hypertrophy with minimal fatigue. Supports running propulsion and hip extension.

**Transfer**: High running transfer through glute strength; the glutes are the primary hip extensor during running.

## Calf Raises

Standing or seated calf raises remain part of the programme but are performed outside the logged routine. They rotate based on fatigue, with the variation and loading adjusted by how the calves feel on the day, so they are not prescribed within the fixed templates.

<ScientificNote>
Plyometric training improves running economy primarily through increased musculotendinous stiffness, the ability of tendons and muscles to store and return elastic energy during ground contact. While formal plyometrics are not programmed in the strength templates, the heavy strength work (for force production) and running itself provide the relevant stimulus for the programme's goals.
</ScientificNote>
