Atlas exists to optimise specific, measurable outcomes. This page defines what those outcomes are and (just as importantly) what they are not.

## Goal Hierarchy

When programming decisions require trade-offs, the following order of importance is used.

| Tier | Priority | Examples |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Tier 1** | Health, Consistency, Longevity | Injury prevention, adherence, sustainable programming |
| **Tier 2** | Running Performance, Physique, Mobility | 2.4 km time, Zone 2 pace, muscular appearance |
| **Tier 3** | Maximum Strength, Specialisation | 1RM performance, single-sport optimisation |

<InfoBox title="How to use this hierarchy">
When a programming decision creates tension between priorities, defer to the higher tier. For example: if a strength exercise improves 1RM but increases injury risk, it should be removed; Tier 1 always defeats Tier 3.
</InfoBox>

## Primary Performance Goal

### 2.4 km Run

The 2.4 km (1.5 mile) run is the primary measurable performance goal. It serves as the North Star for programming decisions.

| Metric | Current | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Time** | 11:40 | 10:30 |
| **Pace** | 4:52/km | 4:22/km |
| **Improvement** | – | 1:10 (10%) |

### Why 2.4 km?

The 2.4 km distance was chosen because it:

- Aligns with IPPT requirements
- Tests both aerobic and anaerobic systems
- Rewards running economy without demanding extreme endurance
- Is short enough to train for without sacrificing muscle mass
- Provides a clear, objective success metric

<CoachNote>
The 2.4 km is a test of **running economy** as much as fitness. Improving technique, cadence, and stiffness will contribute as much to the final time as increased aerobic capacity.
</CoachNote>

## Secondary Performance Goals

### Zone 2 Pace

Improving Zone 2 pace is a proxy for improved aerobic efficiency. A faster Zone 2 at the same heart rate means the aerobic engine is becoming more powerful.

| Metric | Current | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Pace** | ~7:00–7:30/km | 6:30/km |
| **Heart Rate** | ~140–150 bpm | Same or lower |

Zone 2 pace improvement is expected to be **gradual**, measured in months, not weeks.

### Body Composition

Maintain current muscularity while supporting increased endurance performance. No dramatic weight changes are targeted.

| Metric | Current | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Weight** | ~80 kg | ~84 kg |
| **Body Fat** | ~12% | ~12% |

<InfoBox title="Why maintain, not maximise">
Atlas deliberately avoids specialising in bodybuilding or distance running. Extreme physique goals in either direction would compromise the hybrid athlete identity.
</InfoBox>

## Long-Term Vision

The ideal future version of the athlete would:

- Weigh approximately **84 kg** at roughly **12% body fat**
- Run 2.4 km in **10:30**
- Maintain Zone 2 pace of **6:30/km**
- Continue gradual strength progression
- Be noticeably athletic and mobile
- Have excellent posture and movement quality
- Inspire others through consistency rather than intensity

<ScientificNote>
Long-term athletic development research consistently shows that sustainable progress over 5–10 years produces far greater total adaptation than aggressive short-term programming that leads to burnout or injury. The targets above are achievable within 12–18 months of consistent training.
</ScientificNote>

## Non-Goals

Equally important is what Atlas does **not** target:

| Non-Goal | Reason |
|----------|--------|
| Marathon completion | Would compromise muscle mass |
| Powerlifting totals | Not aligned with running goals |
| Bodybuilding stage weight | Unsustainable body fat levels |
| Sub-20 5 km | Would require excessive mileage |
| One-rep max records | Increased injury risk, low transfer |

<WarningBox title="Beware of goal creep">
Adding goals dilutes focus. The programme is designed around a specific set of targets. Resist the temptation to add "just one more" performance goal, each addition creates programming conflicts that weaken the entire system.
</WarningBox>

## Measuring Progress

Progress is measured objectively, not emotionally.

| Quality | Measurement |
|---------|------------|
| 2.4 km time | Timed run (track or measured route) |
| Zone 2 pace | Pace at fixed heart rate (e.g., 145 bpm) |
| Strength | Load at prescribed rep ranges (double progression) |
| Physique | Mirror, measurements, gym performance |
| Recovery | Sleep quality, resting heart rate, subjective readiness |

Progress should be assessed over **4–8 week blocks**, not session to session.

<CoachNote>
Write down your numbers. Memory is unreliable and biased. A simple notes file with dates and key metrics is more valuable than any complex tracking system.
</CoachNote>

## Annual Structure

Atlas follows a repeating annual cycle. Each phase has a distinct focus.

| Phase | Duration | Focus |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **Base Building** | 8–12 weeks | Aerobic development, technique, consistency |
| **Development** | 8–12 weeks | Increase quality sessions, improve running economy |
| **Performance** | 6–8 weeks | Race-specific training, higher intensity |
| **Peak** | 2–4 weeks | Reduce fatigue, maintain intensity (pre-IPPT) |
| **Recovery** | 1–2 weeks | Dissipate accumulated fatigue, prepare next cycle |

Progress between cycles should be marginal and sustainable, not dramatic and risky.
