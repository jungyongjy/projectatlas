The weekly schedule is the structural backbone of Atlas. It is intentionally fixed, not because variation is harmful, but because consistency produces greater long-term results than constant optimisation.

## The Fixed Structure

| Day | Session | Duration | Intensity |
|-----|---------|----------|-----------|
| **Monday** | Full Body A or B | ~30 min | Moderate |
| **Tuesday** | Hard Run | ~30–40 min | High |
| **Wednesday** | Full Body A or B | ~30 min | Moderate |
| **Thursday** | Easy Zone 2 Run | ~30–45 min | Low |
| **Friday** | Full Body A or B | ~30 min | Moderate |
| **Saturday** | Long Run | 45–60 min | Low–Moderate |
| **Sunday** | Complete Rest | – | – |

<InfoBox title="Why this structure?">
Three gym sessions and three runs are balanced across the week, with a complete rest day on Sunday. Only one session is genuinely high-intensity, the Tuesday Hard Run, and it is always performed fresh, preceded by Sunday's complete rest and a Monday gym session with deliberately moderate leg volume. Every demanding session is followed by either an easy session or full rest.
</InfoBox>

## Design Rules

The schedule was designed around five constraints.

### 1. Running Takes Priority

The primary performance goal is the 2.4 km run. Gym sessions must never compromise running quality.

- Tuesday's Hard Run is the most important session of the week and is always performed fresh
- Monday gym keeps leg volume moderate so it cannot compromise the following day's hard run
- Thursday's easy Zone 2 run doubles as a recovery separator between gym sessions
- Saturday's long run is performed at easy effort, so Friday gym, the last of the week, can include leg work

### 2. Low-Fatigue Strength Enables the Structure

A three-gym, three-run week is only sustainable because the strength programme is deliberately low-fatigue. Each gym session is short, machine-dominant, and built from supersets with moderate volume. This is what allows a gym session to sit the day before a run without compromising it.

<CoachNote>
The schedule and the programme are two sides of the same design. Low-fatigue strength work is not a compromise, it is the load-bearing decision that makes concurrent training viable. Every exercise is selected to justify its recovery cost.
</CoachNote>

### 3. Recovery Is Programmed

Hard sessions are never stacked without a recovery window. The two recovery anchors are Thursday's easy Zone 2 run and Sunday's complete rest day.

| Hard Day | Recovery That Follows |
|----------|------------------------|
| Monday Gym | Tuesday Hard Run is a different stimulus; gym leg volume stays moderate |
| Tuesday Hard Run | Wednesday Gym is moderate, short, and includes no quality running |
| Wednesday Gym | Thursday Easy Zone 2 run |
| Friday Gym | Saturday Long Run is a different stimulus at easy effort |
| Saturday Long Run | Sunday Complete Rest |

### 4. Gym Sessions Are Short

Each gym session targets approximately 30 minutes. This is achieved through:

- Supersets (paired exercises, minimal rest)
- Machine-dominant training (low setup time)
- Limited exercise selection (no filler movements)
- Short rest periods (~60 seconds)

A 30-minute session performed consistently three times per week produces far greater annual volume than a 90-minute session performed inconsistently twice per week. The time constraint is a feature, not a limitation.

### 5. The Schedule Is Fixed

The schedule does not change week to week. This is intentional.

- The body adapts to rhythm, not randomness
- Decision fatigue is eliminated; there is no "what should I do today?"
- Habit formation requires repetition
- Consistency produces greater long-term results than constant variation

<ScientificNote>
Research on habit formation and adherence consistently demonstrates that fixed schedules outperform flexible ones for long-term consistency. Every decision, even small ones like "which workout today?", consumes cognitive resources. A fixed schedule eliminates these micro-decisions entirely.
</ScientificNote>

## Why Each Session Exists

Every session has a clear purpose. No session exists "just because."

| Session | Why It Exists |
|---------|---------------|
| Monday Gym | Start the week with a quality strength stimulus |
| Tuesday Hard Run | The key quality session, intervals, threshold, or strides |
| Wednesday Gym | Mid-week strength maintenance |
| Thursday Zone 2 | Aerobic development without fatigue accumulation |
| Friday Gym | Final strength stimulus before the long run |
| Saturday Long Run | Endurance, mental resilience, weekly mileage |
| Sunday Complete Rest | Recovery: programmed, not optional |

## Gym Rotation

Gym sessions rotate between two full-body templates, performed on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.

| Week | Monday | Wednesday | Friday |
|------|--------|-----------|--------|
| Week 1 | A | B | A |
| Week 2 | B | A | B |

This increases exercise frequency (each template is performed 4–5 times per month) while maintaining sufficient variation.

The full exercise lists, superset pairings, and set-and-rep schemes for **Full Body A** and **Full Body B** are maintained as the single source of truth in the [Current Programme](/training/current-programme). This page intentionally does not duplicate them, so the two can never drift apart.

## Running Rotation

Tuesday hard runs rotate through four workout types on a four-week cycle.

| Week | Workout | Focus |
|------|---------|-------|
| Week 1 | 6×400 m Intervals | Speed, VO₂max |
| Week 2 | Norwegian 4×4 | Maximal aerobic stimulus |
| Week 3 | Threshold Run | Lactate threshold |
| Week 4 | Strides Session | Running economy, neuromuscular |

This four-week rotation ensures every major running quality is developed without excessive specialisation in any single domain.

## Daily Non-Negotiables

These habits support the training structure and are treated as seriously as the sessions themselves.

| Habit | Minimum Standard |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Sleep** | 7–8 hours |
| **Hydration** | 2–3 L water |
| **Protein** | 140–160 g |
| **Steps** | ~10,000 |
| **Mobility** | 5–10 minutes |

<WarningBox title="Recovery is programmed, not optional">
Skipping sleep or consistently under-eating protein is equivalent to skipping training sessions. Both reduce adaptation. The programme assumes these habits are maintained, when they are not, the programme should be adjusted (see the Decision Engine).
</WarningBox>

## When to Adjust

The schedule should change only when:

- Life circumstances change permanently (new job, different hours)
- Recovery is consistently inadequate (sleep, stress, hunger)
- An injury requires temporary modification
- A competition phase requires peaking adjustments

The schedule should **not** change because:

- It feels "boring" or "stale"
- A new training trend appears
- Someone you follow trains differently
- You had one bad session

<TipBox>
If you are consistently completing every session and recovering well, the schedule is working. Do not change it. Boredom with a working programme is a discipline problem, not a programming problem.
</TipBox>
