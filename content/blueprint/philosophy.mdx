Atlas is built around one central belief.

> **Consistency beats optimisation.**

Most athletes fail because they continuously search for better programmes rather than executing good ones consistently. Atlas intentionally prioritises sustainability over perfection.

## The Atlas Principles

### Train for Decades

Every decision should still make sense when viewed over the next ten years. Avoid programming decisions that optimise short-term gains while increasing long-term injury risk or burnout.

Ask yourself:

- Can I realistically sustain this?
- Will this still benefit me in five years?
- Does this increase or reduce long-term resilience?

### The Hybrid Athlete

Atlas does not optimise for one sport. Instead, it develops multiple physical qualities simultaneously.

The desired outcome is someone who can:

- Run fast
- Lift reasonably heavy
- Look athletic
- Move well
- Recover efficiently
- Remain healthy

A small compromise in each individual quality is acceptable if overall capability improves.

### Minimum Effective Dose

The objective is not to maximise training volume. It is to maximise adaptation per unit of fatigue.

Every exercise must justify:

- Its recovery cost
- Its time cost
- Its interference with running
- Its contribution toward long-term goals

### High Return on Investment

Exercises should be selected based on value rather than popularity.

High ROI exercises generally satisfy several conditions:

- Easy to progress
- Low injury risk
- High stimulus
- Low fatigue
- Practical
- Transferable

<CoachNote>
Exercises with poor fatigue-to-benefit ratios should be replaced. The best exercise is the one you can do consistently, not the one that looks most impressive.
</CoachNote>

### Consistency Over Novelty

The programme should change slowly. Exercises should not rotate simply because they become boring.

**Novelty is not progression.**

Progression comes from:

- Improved execution
- Increased capacity
- Gradual overload
- Improved movement quality

<ScientificNote>
Research consistently shows that adherence (not programme design) is the strongest predictor of long-term training outcomes. The "perfect" programme that you quit after six weeks is worse than a "good enough" programme you follow for years.
</ScientificNote>
