Recovery nutrition is not a separate category of food. It is normal eating, timed to support the body's adaptation to training. The same foods that form the foundation of daily nutrition — protein, carbohydrates, vegetables, water — are the recovery foods.

## The Three Recovery Priorities

| Priority | What | Why |
|----------|------|-----|
| **1** | Protein | Provides amino acids for muscle protein synthesis and tissue repair |
| **2** | Carbohydrates | Replenishes glycogen depleted during training |
| **3** | Hydration | Replaces fluid lost through sweating |

<InfoBox title="Recovery nutrition is daily nutrition">
There is no special "recovery food" category. The chicken, rice, and vegetables you eat for dinner is recovery nutrition if it follows training. The protein shake you have in the morning supports recovery from yesterday's session. Recovery is not an event — it is the cumulative effect of every meal between sessions.
</InfoBox>

## Session-Specific Recovery

### After Easy Runs (Zone 2, Long Run)

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **Hydration** | Water; replace fluid lost |
| **Next meal** | Normal meal containing protein + carbohydrates |

Easy runs do not significantly deplete glycogen. Normal eating at the next meal is sufficient — no special recovery protocol needed.

### After Quality Runs (Intervals, 4×4, Threshold)

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **Hydration** | Water + optional electrolytes |
| **Within 1–2 hours** | Meal containing 40–50 g protein + carbohydrates |
| **Carbohydrates** | Slightly higher carbohydrate portion than usual — these sessions deplete glycogen |

### After Gym Sessions

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **Protein** | 40–50 g within 1–2 hours |
| **Hydration** | Water |
| **Carbohydrates** | Normal intake — strength training depletes less glycogen than running |

<CoachNote>
The biggest recovery nutrition error is not eating enough after hard sessions. Appetite suppression after intense exercise is common. Plan your post-training meal in advance so that eating is automatic — not dependent on feeling hungry.
</CoachNote>

## Daily Recovery Support

Recovery is not just about the post-training meal. Every meal contributes.

| Factor | Daily Guideline |
|--------|----------------|
| **Protein** | 140–160 g, spread across 3–4 eating occasions |
| **Hydration** | 2–3 L water, consistently throughout the day |
| **Fruits and vegetables** | 3–5 servings; vitamins, minerals, antioxidants |
| **Sleep** | 7–8 hours (the most important recovery variable) |

## Alcohol

| Context | Guideline |
|---------|-----------|
| **Rest days** | Occasional moderate intake (1–2 drinks) — minimal impact on recovery |
| **Training days** | Avoid immediately post-training; alcohol impairs glycogen replenishment and muscle protein synthesis |
| **Quality run days** | Avoid entirely — alcohol degrades sleep quality, which compounds the recovery demand from intense training |
| **Race week** | Avoid entirely |

<WarningBox title="Alcohol and recovery">
Alcohol impairs muscle protein synthesis, reduces sleep quality (particularly REM and deep sleep), and delays glycogen replenishment. Occasional moderate intake on rest days has minimal long-term impact. Regular intake — particularly after training sessions — measurably impairs adaptation. The dose and timing determine the cost.
</WarningBox>

## Common Mistakes

### Overcomplicating Recovery Nutrition

Recovery nutrition is not a special category of products and protocols. It is normal food, eaten consistently. You do not need recovery shakes, specialised supplements, or precise nutrient timing protocols. Eat a meal with protein and carbohydrates within a couple of hours of training. That is sufficient.

### Relying on Supplements Instead of Food

A protein shake after training is convenient. It should not replace a meal. Whole foods provide additional nutrients — vitamins, minerals, phytochemicals — that protein powder does not contain. Use whey for convenience; eat food for recovery.

<TipBox>
The best recovery meal is one you will actually eat. If you finish a hard session at 8 PM and the choice is between a carefully prepared meal that takes 45 minutes or a simple meal in 10 minutes, choose the simple meal. Timely nutrition beats delayed perfect nutrition.
</TipBox>
