Travel disrupts nutrition routines. The goal during travel is not perfection — it is maintaining the habits that matter most while accepting that some variables are outside your control.

## The Travel Nutrition Hierarchy

When travel makes normal eating impossible, prioritise in this order:

| Priority | Target | Why |
|----------|--------|-----|
| **1** | Protein | Most important; hardest to find in convenience food |
| **2** | Hydration | Travel (especially flights) is dehydrating |
| **3** | Calories | Eat enough; undereating during travel impairs training |
| **4** | Food quality | Whole foods preferred; convenience acceptable temporarily |

<InfoBox title="Perfect is the enemy of adequate">
A few days of suboptimal nutrition has negligible long-term impact. The stress of trying to eat perfectly while travelling often causes more harm (skipping meals, over-restricting) than simply accepting "good enough" choices.
</InfoBox>

## Practical Strategies

### Protein While Travelling

| Strategy | Example |
|----------|---------|
| **Pack protein powder** | Single-serving sachets or a small container; add water anywhere |
| **Choose protein at every meal** | Hotel breakfast: eggs, yoghurt. Airport: chicken sandwich, protein bar |
| **Convenience store protein** | Greek yoghurt, milk, hard-boiled eggs, protein bars, jerky |
| **Restaurant strategy** | Order dishes built around meat, fish, or eggs; add vegetables |

### Hydration While Travelling

| Strategy | Example |
|----------|---------|
| **Carry an empty water bottle** | Fill after security at airports; refill throughout the day |
| **Drink before you are thirsty** | Travel is distracting; thirst signals are easy to miss |
| **Limit alcohol on travel days** | Alcohol at altitude (flying) is more dehydrating than at sea level |
| **Rehydrate on arrival** | 500–750 ml within an hour of reaching your destination |

### Eating Enough

| Situation | Strategy |
|-----------|----------|
| **Long flights** | Eat the meals provided; bring snacks (nuts, protein bar, fruit) |
| **Busy work travel** | Eat when food is available, even if not at normal meal times |
| **Limited options** | Quantity over quality — eating enough of "acceptable" food is better than undereating while searching for "good" food |
| **Jet lag** | Eat at local meal times on arrival, even if not hungry |

<CoachNote>
The biggest travel nutrition mistake is undereating. Busy schedules, unfamiliar environments, and disrupted routines suppress appetite and make food access inconvenient. Accept that food quality may be lower than at home. Prioritise eating enough — especially protein — over eating perfectly.
</CoachNote>

## Training While Travelling

If travel includes training sessions:

| Consideration | Strategy |
|--------------|----------|
| **Pre-run fuel** | Banana or small snack from hotel or convenience store |
| **Post-run protein** | Whey protein (packed) or hotel breakfast |
| **Hydration** | Extra water before and after running in unfamiliar climates |
| **Gym access** | Hotel gyms are limited; prioritise maintenance over progression |

## Supplements While Travelling

| Supplement | Travel Strategy |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Whey protein** | Pack in checked luggage or buy single-serve sachets |
| **Creatine** | 5 g daily; pack in a small container or sachets; if unavailable for a few days, resume on return — no loading needed |

<TipBox>
Pack a "nutrition kit" for travel: whey protein sachets, creatine (pre-portioned), a few protein bars, and an empty water bottle. This covers protein and hydration — the two hardest things to maintain while travelling — with minimal space and effort.
</TipBox>

## Returning Home

Resume normal nutrition habits immediately upon return. Do not "compensate" for travel eating by restricting intake — this creates an unnecessary cycle of restriction and overeating. Travel was a temporary deviation; home is the return to normal. Treat it that way.
