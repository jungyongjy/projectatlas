Hydration is the most underrated performance variable. Even mild dehydration (2% body mass loss) measurably degrades both endurance and strength performance. The solution is simple: drink enough water, consistently.

## Daily Target

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Daily water intake** | 2–3 L |
| **Current intake** | ~2 L |
| **Goal** | Consistent 2.5–3 L |

<InfoBox title="2–3 litres is a guideline, not a prescription">
Water needs vary with temperature, training volume, sweat rate, and diet. The goal is pale yellow urine — not clear (overhydrated) and not dark (dehydrated). Urine colour is a more reliable indicator than a fixed volume target.
</InfoBox>

## Why Hydration Matters

| Function | Effect of Dehydration |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Endurance performance** | Degraded at 2% body mass loss; significant impairment at 3%+ |
| **Strength performance** | Reduced force production capacity |
| **Cognitive function** | Impaired concentration, decision-making, and perceived effort |
| **Recovery** | Slower nutrient delivery to tissues, reduced waste clearance |
| **Thermoregulation** | Reduced sweating capacity, increased core temperature during exercise |
| **Joint health** | Synovial fluid viscosity affected by hydration status |

## Practical Strategy

### Throughout the Day

| Time | Guideline |
|------|-----------|
| **Morning** | 500 ml within 30 minutes of waking |
| **Mid-morning** | 500 ml by lunch |
| **Afternoon** | 500 ml between lunch and end of work |
| **Evening** | 500 ml with and after dinner |
| **Total** | ~2–2.5 L baseline, plus training hydration |

### Around Training

| Timing | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pre-training** | 300–500 ml in the hour before |
| **During training** | Sips as needed; for sessions under 60 minutes, pre-hydration is usually sufficient |
| **Post-training** | Replace fluid lost (~500–750 ml per hour of training) |

<TipBox>
Carry a water bottle. Having water visible and accessible increases intake without conscious effort. A 750 ml bottle that needs to be refilled 3–4 times per day is a simple system. No app, no tracking — just an empty bottle that needs refilling.
</TipBox>

## Electrolytes

For most sessions, plain water is sufficient. Electrolyte supplementation is worth considering for:

- Sessions over 60 minutes
- Training in high heat and humidity
- Days with unusually high sweat loss
- After runs where visible salt residue is present on skin or clothing

A simple electrolyte solution: a pinch of salt in water with a squeeze of lemon or a commercial electrolyte tablet. No need for high-sugar sports drinks for sessions under 60 minutes.

<CoachNote>
If you are thirsty, you are already mildly dehydrated. Thirst is a lagging indicator. Drink water consistently throughout the day rather than reacting to thirst. The goal is to never feel thirsty.
</CoachNote>

## Common Mistakes

### Drinking Only When Thirsty

Thirst indicates dehydration has already begun. Regular, habitual water intake prevents reaching the point of thirst.

### Overhydrating Before Training

Drinking a litre of water immediately before a run creates gastrointestinal discomfort without improving hydration status. Hydrate throughout the day; small amounts in the hour before training.

### Ignoring Hydration on Rest Days

Hydration needs do not disappear on rest days. Recovery processes — tissue repair, nutrient delivery, waste clearance — all require adequate hydration.

<ScientificNote>
A 2% loss of body mass through dehydration reduces endurance performance by approximately 5–10% in temperate conditions and up to 20% in hot conditions. Cognitive performance (decision-making, concentration) is impaired at similar thresholds. For a 2.4 km run lasting ~11 minutes, a 5% performance decrement translates to approximately 35 seconds — the difference between a personal best and a disappointing result.
</ScientificNote>
