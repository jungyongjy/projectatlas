Calorie intake should support training performance and recovery while maintaining body composition. The objective is **energy availability** (having enough fuel to train hard and recover fully) not weight loss or gain.

## Daily Target

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated intake** | ~2,000–2,500 kcal/day |
| **Goal** | Slight surplus or maintenance |
| **Tracking method** | Intuitive (no calorie counting) |

<InfoBox title="Why no calorie counting?">
Calorie tracking is effective for specific short-term goals (weight loss, competition preparation) but is unsustainable and unnecessary for long-term athletic development. The recommendations below are designed to regulate intake through habits rather than measurement.
</InfoBox>

## Energy Balance Principles

### Signs You Are Eating Enough

- Consistent training performance (not declining week to week)
- Stable body weight (fluctuations of 1–2 kg are normal, not a trend)
- Adequate energy throughout the day
- Quality sleep (undereating disrupts sleep)
- Normal hunger cues (not constantly hungry or never hungry)

### Signs You May Be Undereating

- Persistent fatigue not explained by training load
- Declining gym or running performance
- Poor recovery between sessions
- Irritability or low motivation
- Frequent illness or slow-healing injuries
- Disrupted sleep (waking hungry, unable to fall back asleep)

<WarningBox title="Undereating and training">
Training in a significant energy deficit degrades performance, increases injury risk, and impairs recovery. The body conserves energy by reducing non-essential functions, including muscle protein synthesis and immune response. For a hybrid athlete, adequate energy intake is a performance requirement, not a cosmetic concern.
</WarningBox>

## Practical Guidelines

### Meal Structure

| Meal | Approximate Calories | Notes |
|------|---------------------|-------|
| Breakfast (protein shake) | ~200–300 | Quick, convenient, adequate |
| Lunch | ~700–900 | Largest meal of the day |
| Dinner | ~700–900 | Similar to lunch |
| Snacks (if any) | ~200–300 | Fruit, yoghurt, nuts |
| **Total** | **~2,000–2,500** | |

### Adjusting Intake

| Goal | Adjustment |
|------|-----------|
| **Maintain weight, improve performance** | Current intake is likely appropriate |
| **Slowly gain muscle** | Add ~200–300 kcal/day via protein or carbohydrates |
| **Feeling under-recovered** | Increase carbohydrates around training before increasing total calories |

<CoachNote>
Do not overthink calories. Eat three meals a day. Ensure each contains adequate protein. Eat enough to fuel training and recover. Adjust based on how you feel and perform, not based on a calculator. Your body is a more accurate energy balance sensor than any app.
</CoachNote>

## Common Mistakes

### Undereating Protein and Overeating Everything Else

Protein is the most satiating macronutrient. Adequate protein intake naturally regulates appetite. If protein is low, hunger drives overconsumption of less satiating foods (refined carbohydrates, fats) to compensate.

### Eating Too Little on Training Days

Training increases energy expenditure. Some athletes unconsciously eat less on training days (busy, not hungry immediately after exercise), creating a deficit when energy demand is highest. Prioritise post-training nutrition even if not immediately hungry.

### Compensating for Training with Food Restriction

"I ran today, so I should eat less." The opposite is true: training days require more energy, not less. Restricting intake after training impairs recovery and adaptation.

<TipBox>
Weigh yourself at most once per week, same day, same time, same conditions. Body weight fluctuates 1–2 kg daily due to hydration, glycogen, and gut content. Daily weigh-ins create noise without signal.
</TipBox>
